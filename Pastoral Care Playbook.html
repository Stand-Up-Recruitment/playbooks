Non-Negotiables
1 · Nothing moves until TOB is signed AND invoice is paid
No relocation work begins before this gate is cleared. No exceptions.
2 · Accommodation is booked before flights — always
Accommodation can fall through. Flights are easy and go every day. Never book flights first.
3 · Support Team owns the relationship post-handover
Recruiter is out. Any attempt to re-engage the recruiter goes back to Support Team. No exceptions.
4 · Every check-in must be logged in JobAdder with a health score
If it is not logged, it did not happen. This protects SUR if a placement is ever disputed.
5 · Flights booked within week 1 of placement — 3 weeks before start date
All candidates have 4 weeks from placement to start. Clients need flight confirmation as proof of commitment.
6 · Candidate must arrive at least 3 days before start date
Non-negotiable for both pathways. Communicate this clearly on the Documents Call.

Concern Resolution Protocol
Applies from Week 2 onward, any check-in, either direction — client→candidate or candidate→client. This is separate from the escalation-to-Les scenarios below; most concerns resolve here and never need to go further.
Week 1 stays light. Simple call; no pickup → email. Don't dig for issues yet — both sides are still settling in.
StepAction
1Call. Ask what's going well first, then what's not.
2If a concern comes up — tell them you'll contact the other side to discuss it. Don't try to resolve it on this call.
3Contact the other party (whoever the concern is about).
4With them: start with the good, then raise the concern. Close by asking "what's the plan?" — put it back on them to propose the solution, don't hand them one.
5Go back to the original person. Relay what the other side said and how it's going to work out. Make them feel heard.
6Close the loop — send a written overview email of what was discussed, CC'ing both client and candidate.
Standing rule: If a concern is raised over email, always call — don't resolve it in writing. No answer on the call → follow up by email asking when's a good time to ring.

Guarantee Claim Process
Guarantee window: 4 months from candidate start date. This is what actually happens when a client invokes it — typically triggered by the "client terminates" or "candidate quits" scenarios above.
StepAction
1Confirm the claim is inside the 4-month window from the candidate's start date.
2Escalate to Les same day.
3Les determines validity and presents the client with their choice — refund or replacement candidate.
4 — if refundLes → Nihanga processes via Xero.
4 — if replacementLes/Nihanga handle the commercial side (contract amendment, any fee adjustment). Recruiter (Ian or Ayn) sources the replacement candidate.
5Log the outcome in JobAdder — client's choice, date, resolution.
Pre-Departure Operational Scenarios
Logistics failures before the candidate lands — separate from the concern/conflict scenarios above, but they follow the same rule: something time-critical gets Les's visibility fast, not at the next scheduled catch-up.
ScenarioProtocol
Candidate pulls outLog in JobAdder and notify recruiter same day. Recruiter notifies client within 24 hours. Escalate to Les same day regardless of reason — revenue impact needs his visibility immediately. Les determines refund/replacement per contract terms with client.

Log the incident with the transfer provider same day. Second no-show ever from the same provider → escalate to Les to review the provider relationship.

Post-arrival (host backs out after landing): urgent, same-day — book a temporary hotel while re-searching, escalate to Les immediately given cost and candidate welfare.
Flights not booked by deadlineDay 5, no flights booked → call the candidate directly, understand the blocker. Day 7 (deadline), still not booked → escalate to recruiter — this is now a client-relationship risk. Recruiter unresolved within 24 hours → escalate to Les.
Property manager unresponsiveNo response within 24–48 hours → run 2 more properties in parallel rather than waiting single-threaded on one. Deal falls through inside 2 weeks of the candidate's flight → escalate to Les.
Trade licensing not actioned / rejectedCheck at the Week 1 check-in that the application has been started. No action taken 3 weeks out from start date → escalate to recruiter, client may need a heads-up if the licence is required to legally start on site. Trade Licensing — NZ to Australia

State-by-state licensing requirements for NZ tradespeople working in Australia. TTMRA is not automatic — candidates must formally apply before starting work.

Required Documents — All Applications
Proof of identity (passport, driver's licence, birth certificate — 100 points of ID)
Current NZ licence — must be valid at time of application
2 recent passport photos
Unique Student Identifier (USI) — free at usi.gov.au
Payment method (fees vary by state)
Certified copies where required (JP or solicitor)
National Requirements — All States
Required for all sites
White Card (CPCWHS1001)
Required for everyone on an Australian construction site. NZ Site Safe cards are NOT accepted.
Steps: 1. Create USI at usi.gov.au · 2. Find RTO at training.gov.au or complete online at whitecardonline.com.au · 3. Complete CPCWHS1001 — 1 day in-person or a few hours online (~$100-150) · 4. Verify identity with RTO · 5. White Card posted within a few weeks. Valid for life, recognised in all states.

VIC candidates: online delivery isn't accepted — Victoria requires this to be completed in person. Book a face-to-face provider before flying if the candidate is placed in VIC.
Applicable trades
High Risk Work Licences (HRWL)
Required for: Scaffolding, Rigging, Cranes, Forklifts, EWPs, Pressure Equipment. Nationally recognised.
Steps: 1. Locate NZ qualification certificate for relevant class · 2. Find Australian RTO at training.gov.au · 3. Book skills assessment · 4. Complete any gap training required · 5. RTO submits application to state regulator.
Trans-Tasman Mutual Recognition (TTMRA)
Legal agreement allowing NZ trade licences to be recognised in Australia. NOT automatic — must formally apply before starting work. NZ licence must be current. Apply state-by-state.
Automatic Mutual Recognition (AMR)
Applies to Australian state licences only — NOT NZ licences. Once a candidate has an Australian licence they can work in NSW, VIC, WA, SA, TAS, NT without re-applying. QLD does NOT participate in AMR. ACT excludes construction trades until July 2027.
