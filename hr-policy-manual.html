Mental Health Wellbeing Policy

1. Purpose

Stand Up Recruitment Limited is committed to creating a workplace where all employees, contractors, and volunteers feel supported, valued, and psychologically safe. This policy establishes our commitment to promoting mental health and wellbeing as an integral part of our health and safety obligations under New Zealand law.

Mental health is as important as physical health. We recognise that our people are our most valuable asset, and their wellbeing directly impacts our ability to deliver excellent service to our candidates and clients.

2. Scope

This policy applies to:

  • All permanent and fixed-term employees of Stand Up Recruitment Limited
  • Contractors and consultants engaged by Stand Up Recruitment
  • Volunteer interns and placement students
  • All work locations, including remote and hybrid work arrangements
3. Legislative Framework

This policy has been developed in accordance with the following New Zealand legislation:

  • Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (HSWA) – requires all employers (PCBUs) to eliminate or minimise risks to worker health, including psychosocial risks and mental health hazards
  • Employment Relations Act 2000 – requires good faith obligations and imposes a duty of care toward employees
  • Human Rights Act 1993 – prohibits discrimination on the grounds of disability, including mental illness
  • Privacy Act 2020 – governs the handling of sensitive personal health information
  • Holidays Act 2003 – supports work-life balance through minimum leave entitlements
4. Our Commitment

Stand Up Recruitment Limited commits to:

  • Actively promoting a positive, psychologically safe work environment
  • Identifying and managing work-related psychosocial hazards (e.g. excessive workload, poor work design, role ambiguity, interpersonal conflict)
  • Providing reasonable support and adjustments to employees experiencing mental health challenges
  • Treating mental health disclosures with confidentiality, sensitivity, and without stigma
  • Training leaders and managers to recognise early signs of distress and respond appropriately
  • Encouraging open conversations about mental health without fear of judgment or adverse consequences
  • Connecting employees with appropriate professional support resources
5. Responsibilities
5.1 The Company (PCBU)
  • Ensure this policy is implemented, communicated, and reviewed regularly
  • Allocate appropriate resources to support employee wellbeing initiatives
  • Monitor workloads, work design, and team dynamics for psychosocial risks
  • Ensure managers are trained in mental health awareness and their obligations under this policy
  • Take prompt action when mental health concerns or risks are identified
5.2 Leaders and Managers
  • Model healthy work behaviours, including taking breaks and respecting personal boundaries
  • Create a team environment where employees feel comfortable raising concerns
  • Monitor workloads and check in regularly with team members
  • Respond to disclosures with empathy, confidentiality, and without judgment
  • Escalate concerns to the HR Lead where appropriate and in a timely manner
  • Participate in mental health awareness training as required
5.3 Employees
  • Take reasonable care of their own health and wellbeing
  • Seek support early when experiencing difficulties, before issues escalate
  • Treat colleagues with respect and kindness, contributing to a positive team culture
  • Participate in wellbeing initiatives provided by the company
  • Report any workplace concerns that may be affecting their mental health through appropriate channels
6. Psychosocial Hazard Management

Under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, Stand Up Recruitment is required to identify and manage psychosocial hazards in the workplace. These include, but are not limited to:

  • Excessive or unrealistic workloads and time pressure
  • Lack of role clarity or conflicting demands
  • Low job control or autonomy
  • Poor workplace relationships or interpersonal conflict
  • Exposure to traumatic, distressing, or emotionally demanding content or interactions
  • Job insecurity or significant organisational change
  • Isolation, particularly in remote working arrangements

We will identify, assess, and manage these hazards through our regular health and safety review processes and employee check-ins.

7. Mental Health Support
7.1 Internal Support

Employees are encouraged to speak with their direct manager or the HR if they are experiencing difficulties. All conversations will be treated in confidence unless there is a serious safety concern.

7.2 External Resources

Stand Up Recruitment encourages employees to access the following free, confidential support services available in New Zealand:

7.3 Reasonable Adjustments

Where an employee is experiencing a mental health condition, Stand Up Recruitment will consider reasonable adjustments, including:

  • Temporary modification of duties or workload
  • Flexible start and finish times
  • Approved leave arrangements in accordance with the Holidays Act 2003
  • Regular check-in meetings with the HR or manager
8. Leave and Return to Work

Employees who require time away from work due to a mental health condition may be entitled to sick leave under the Holidays Act 2003. Where sick leave has been exhausted, additional unpaid leave may be considered on a case-by-case basis.

Stand Up Recruitment will support a planned and gradual return to work following a mental health-related absence, in a manner agreed with the employee and any treating health professional.

9. Confidentiality

All mental health disclosures will be treated with the utmost confidentiality in accordance with the Privacy Act 2020. Information will only be disclosed to others on a need-to-know basis and only with the employee’s informed consent, except where required by law or where there is a serious and imminent risk to health and safety.

10. Anti-Stigma and Non-Discrimination

Stand Up Recruitment will not tolerate stigma, discrimination, or negative treatment of any employee on the basis of their mental health status. Under the Human Rights Act 1993, mental illness is recognised as a disability. Any discriminatory behaviour will be treated as a serious disciplinary matter.

Employees are encouraged to report any concerns regarding stigma or discrimination to the HR Lead or the Founder directly.

11. Wellbeing Initiatives

Stand Up Recruitment will, where practical, implement proactive wellbeing initiatives, including:

  • Regular team check-ins and one-on-one conversations
  • Encouraging the use of annual leave and discouraging excessive unpaid overtime
  • Flexible working arrangements where operationally feasible
  • Acknowledging and celebrating team contributions and achievements
  • Access to mental health awareness resources and information
12. Policy Review

This policy will be reviewed annually, or following any significant organisational change, legislative update, or workplace incident that indicates a need for review. All employees will be notified of any material changes.

Bullying Harassment Prevention Policy

1. Introduction

Stand Up Recruitment is committed to the provision of a fair, healthy and safe workplace in which everyone is treated with dignity and respect and in which no individual or group feels bullied, threatened or intimidated.

The intention of these procedures is to inform workers of the type of behaviour that is unacceptable and to provide procedural guidance. We recognise that we have a duty to implement this policy and all workers are expected to comply with it.

Bullying or harassment in any form is unacceptable behaviour and will not be permitted or condoned.

Stand Up Recruitment recognises that bullying and harassment can exist in the workplace, as well as outside, and that this can seriously affect worker’s working lives by detracting from a productive working environment and can impact on the health, confidence, morale and performance of those affected by it, including anyone who witnesses or has knowledge of the unwanted or unacceptable behaviour.

2. Scope

This policy applies to:

  • All permanent and fixed-term employees
  • Contractors, consultants, and agency workers
  • Volunteer interns and placement students
  • Visitors, clients, candidates, and third parties interacting with our team

This policy covers conduct occurring in the workplace, at work-related events and social functions, during business travel, and via digital channels including email, messaging platforms, and social media.

3. Legislative Framework
  • Employment Relations Act 2000 – requires good faith and provides grounds for personal grievance claims relating to harassment
  • Human Rights Act 1993 – prohibits discrimination and harassment on protected grounds
  • Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 – requires employers to identify and manage psychosocial hazards including bullying
  • Harmful Digital Communications Act 2015 – addresses harmful electronic communications including cyberbullying
  • Privacy Act 2020 – governs the handling of personal information during investigations
4. Definitions
4.1 Harassment

Harassment is any unwanted physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct based on grounds of age, disability, gender identity, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy or maternity, political opinion, family status, race, religion or belief, sex or sexual orientation which affects the dignity of anyone at work or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment.

A single incident of unwanted or offensive behaviour can amount to harassment.

Examples of harassment include:

  • Insensitive jokes and pranks
  • Lewd or abusive comments about appearance
  • Deliberate exclusion from conversations
  • Displaying abusive or offensive writing or material
  • Unwelcome touching
  • Abusive, threatening or insulting words or behaviour
4.2 Bullying

Bullying is repeated, offensive, abusive, intimidating, insulting or unreasonable behaviour directed towards an individual or a group, which makes the recipient(s) feel threatened, humiliated or vulnerable. Single incidents of bullying will not be tolerated.

Bullying can occur in the workplace and outside of the workplace at events connected to the workplace, such as social functions or business trips.

Examples of bullying include:

  • Abusive, insulting or offensive language or comments
  • Unjustified criticism or complaints
  • Physical or emotional threats
  • Deliberate exclusion from workplace activities
  • The spreading of misinformation or malicious rumours
  • The denial of access to information, supervision or resources that has a detrimental impact on the individual or group
4.3 What This Policy Does Not Cover

The following are not considered bullying or harassment:

  • Reasonable and lawful management action, including performance management and disciplinary processes
  • Constructive feedback given respectfully and professionally
  • A single incident of rudeness or insensitivity (unless severe enough to constitute harassment on its own)
5. Responsibilities
5.1 The Company
  • Implement and promote this policy across the organisation
  • Investigate all complaints promptly, fairly, and confidentially
  • Take appropriate disciplinary action where bullying or harassment is substantiated
  • Protect complainants and witnesses from retaliation
5.2 Leaders and Managers
  • Model respectful, professional behaviour at all times
  • Address inappropriate behaviour promptly, even where no formal complaint has been made
  • Escalate complaints to the HR Lead immediately upon becoming aware of an issue
  • Maintain confidentiality throughout any complaint or investigation
5.3 Employees
  • Treat all colleagues, clients, and candidates with dignity and respect
  • Report any incidents they experience or witness
  • Cooperate honestly in any investigation process
  • Maintain confidentiality throughout any complaint or investigation
6. Complaint Procedures
6.1 Informal Complaint

Stand Up Recruitment recognises that complaints of bullying, harassment, and particularly of sexual harassment, can sometimes be of a sensitive or intimate nature and that it may not be appropriate to raise the issue through the normal grievance procedure. In these circumstances, workers are encouraged to raise such issues with a senior colleague of their choice as a confidential helper.

If you are the victim of minor bullying or harassment you should make it clear to the alleged bully or harasser on an informal basis that their behaviour is unwelcome and ask the individual to stop. If you feel unable to do this verbally then you should hand a written request to the individual.

6.2 Formal Complaint

Where the informal approach fails or if the bullying or harassment is more serious, you should bring the matter to the attention of Stand Up Recruitment as a formal written complaint. If possible, you should keep notes of the bullying or harassment so that the written complaint can include:

  • The name of the alleged bully or harasser
  • The nature of the alleged incident of bullying or harassment
  • The dates and times when the alleged incident occurred
  • The names of any witnesses
  • Any action already taken by you to stop the alleged bullying or harassment
7. Investigation Process

On receipt of a formal complaint, Stand Up Recruitment will take action to separate you from the alleged bully or harasser to enable an uninterrupted investigation. This may involve a temporary transfer or suspension with contractual pay.

StepAction
Step 1Complaint received and acknowledged within 2 business days
Step 2Assessment of complaint and interim measures if required
Step 3Respondent notified and given opportunity to respond
Step 4Interviews conducted with all relevant parties
Step 5Written findings prepared and both parties informed
Step 6Appropriate resolution and disciplinary action if warranted

The person dealing with the complaint will invite you to attend a meeting at a reasonable time and location. You have the right to be accompanied by a confidential helper and/or a support person. Those involved in the investigation will be expected to act in confidence and any breach of confidence may be a disciplinary matter.

On conclusion of the investigation, which will normally be within ten working days of the meeting, a report of the findings and any decision will be sent in writing to you and to the alleged bully or harasser.

8. Confidentiality

All parties involved in a complaint or investigation are required to maintain confidentiality throughout. Information will only be shared on a need-to-know basis in accordance with the Privacy Act 2020. A breach of confidentiality during an investigation may itself constitute misconduct.

9. Protection from Retaliation

Stand Up Recruitment strictly prohibits any form of retaliation against a person who has made a complaint in good faith, participated in an investigation as a witness, or supported another person in making a complaint.

If you bring a complaint of bullying or harassment you will not be victimised for having brought the complaint. However, if the report concludes that the complaint is both untrue and has been brought with malicious intent, appropriate action will be taken against you.

10. Disciplinary Consequences

Where bullying or harassment is substantiated, disciplinary action may include:

  • A formal written warning
  • Required participation in training or counselling
  • Reassignment of duties or reporting lines
  • Suspension pending investigation
  • Termination of employment for serious or repeated misconduct
11. External Reporting

Employees not satisfied with the outcome of an internal investigation may raise a personal grievance under the Employment Relations Act 2000 within 90 calendar days of the act or omission.

1. Purpose

This policy sets out the leave entitlements available to employees of Stand Up Recruitment Limited and the procedures for requesting, approving, and recording leave. All leave must be applied for and approved in accordance with this policy.

2. Scope

This policy applies to all permanent, fixed-term, and casual employees of Stand Up Recruitment Limited, unless otherwise specified in individual employment agreements.

3. Annual Leave
3.1 Entitlement

Employees are entitled to accrue annual leave in accordance with the Holidays Act 2003, unless otherwise stated in the employment agreement. It is the Employer’s policy to encourage employees to take all annual leave entitlement in the current leave year.

3.2 Requesting Annual Leave
  • All leave requests must be submitted through Xero and approved by management before any firm holiday arrangements are made
  • A minimum of two weeks’ notice is required when requesting annual leave
  • Annual leave dates are allocated on a first come, first served basis, subject to maintaining operational efficiency and appropriate staffing levels
  • The Employer may not be able to accommodate requests during peak or busy periods
3.3 Payment

Unless otherwise agreed, annual holiday pay will be paid in the normal pay cycle that relates to the period in which the leave is taken.

3.4 Annual Closedown

The Employer or client may choose to temporarily close down, for instance during the end-of-year period. Where practical, at least 14 days’ notice will be provided. Employees are required to reserve sufficient annual leave to cover the closedown period. If insufficient annual leave has been accrued, the shortfall will be taken as unpaid leave.

3.5 Public Holidays

Entitlement to public holidays is in accordance with the Holidays Act 2003. Due to the nature of the business, employees may be reasonably required to work on a public holiday. Advance notice will be provided where this is required.

4. Birthday Leave
4.1 Entitlement

Stand Up Recruitment recognises and celebrates each employee as an individual. All permanent employees who have completed their first six months of continuous employment are entitled to one day of paid Birthday Leave per year.

4.2 Taking Birthday Leave
  • Birthday Leave may be taken on the employee’s actual birthday or within five working days before or after the birthday date
  • The leave must be requested through Xero and approved by management in advance
  • A minimum of five working days’ notice is required
  • Birthday Leave cannot be accumulated or carried over to the following year if not taken
4.3 Working on Your Birthday – Leave Encashment

If an employee chooses to work on their birthday and the Birthday Leave is not taken within the five working day window, the employee may elect to encash the leave day:

  • The employee must notify the HR Lead in writing of their election to encash within 10 working days of the birthday
  • Encashment will be paid at the employee’s ordinary daily rate of pay and processed in the next available pay cycle
  • Once an election to encash has been made, it cannot be reversed
  • Birthday Leave encashment is not available where Birthday Leave has already been taken in that leave year
5. Sick Leave
5.1 Entitlement

Permanent employees are entitled to up to ten days of paid sick leave per year of continuous service, accessible after six months of continuous service, in accordance with the Holidays Act 2003. Fixed-term and casual employees are entitled to sick leave provided they meet the minimum working hours test under the Holidays Act 2003.

Sick leave may be taken where an employee:

  • Is not fit for work due to a personal illness or injury
  • Needs to provide care or support to a spouse, partner, or dependent due to illness or injury
5.2 Notification
  • The Employer must be notified by telephone or text on the first day of absence, no later than one hour before the usual start time
  • An expected return date should be provided and updated as necessary
  • Notification procedures must be followed each day of absence unless a medical certificate covers the period
  • If incapacity extends beyond seven days, the Employer must be notified once a week unless otherwise agreed
5.3 Evidence of Incapacity

A medical certificate from a registered health practitioner is required for any absence exceeding three consecutive days. The Employer may require a medical certificate for any single day absence and will notify the employee accordingly.

5.4 Return to Work

Employees must notify their manager of their return date as soon as known. A return to work interview may be required following a period of sick leave. Where an employee has suffered a workplace injury or illness that required medical treatment, a doctor’s certificate confirming fitness to return will always be required. Employees with an infectious or contagious illness must not report for work without medical clearance.

6. Parental Leave
6.1 Primary Carer Leave

Employees who are the primary carer of a child may be entitled to primary carer leave under the Parental Leave and Employment Protection Act 1987, provided they have worked an average of at least 10 hours per week in the six or 12 months immediately prior to the expected date of delivery. Primary carer leave must be taken in one continuous period not exceeding 26 weeks.

6.2 Notice Requirements

Employees wishing to take parental leave must notify the Employer at least three months before the expected birth date, providing:

  • A medical certificate indicating the expected delivery date
  • The proposed start date of leave
  • The duration of leave requested
  • An expected return date
  • Details of any parental leave the partner intends to take
6.3 Partner’s Leave

A spouse or partner may be entitled to partner’s leave subject to meeting the six or 12 month employment test. Employees should notify the Employer as early as possible.

6.4 Payment

All parental leave is unpaid unless otherwise specified in the individual employment agreement. Employees may be eligible for government-funded paid parental leave through Inland Revenue.

6.5 Position Protection

Where an employee takes parental leave not exceeding four weeks, the Employer will hold their position open for the duration, except where a redundancy occurs. For leave exceeding four weeks, the Employer will determine whether it is reasonably practicable to hold the position open in accordance with the Parental Leave and Employment Protection Act 1987.

7. Bereavement Leave

Employees are entitled to bereavement leave in accordance with the Holidays Act 2003:

CircumstanceEntitlement
Death of spouse/partner, parent, child, sibling, grandparent, grandchild, or spouse’s/partner’s parent3 days paid
End of pregnancy by miscarriage or stillbirth (specified circumstances)3 days paid
Death of any other person where the Employer accepts a bereavement has occurredUp to 1 day paid

Fixed-term and casual employees must meet the minimum working hours test under the Holidays Act 2003 to be entitled to bereavement leave.

8. Family Violence Leave

Employees affected by family violence are entitled to up to 10 days’ paid family violence leave per 12-month period of continuous employment, after the initial six months of employment, in accordance with the Holidays Act 2003.

Employees may also request short-term flexible working arrangements (up to two months). Requests must be made in writing. The Employer reserves the right to request proof of family violence.

9. Jury Service

Employees summoned for jury service will be provided with unpaid time off to complete the service. The Employer must be notified as soon as practicable after receiving a summons. Where not chosen for jury service or upon completion, employees must return to work promptly.

10. Time Off for Appointments

Where possible, medical, dental, or other personal appointments should be scheduled outside normal working hours. Where this is not possible, time off may be granted at the Employer’s discretion and will normally be without pay.

11. Leave Records

All leave is to be recorded and managed through Xero. Employees are responsible for ensuring leave requests are submitted and approved in advance. Any discrepancies in leave records must be raised with the HR Lead promptly.

12. Policy Review

This policy will be reviewed annually or following any significant legislative change. Employees will be notified of any material amendments.

1. Purpose

This policy sets out the Employer’s expectations regarding hours of work, rest and meal breaks, punctuality, and absence management. All employees are expected to attend work reliably and on time.

2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees of Stand Up Recruitment Limited.

3. Hours of Work

Employees may be required to work Monday through Friday. Current usual business hours are 7:00am to 4:00pm, though these may change from time to time. Specific hours of work will be set out in each employee’s employment agreement.

4. Rest and Meal Breaks

Rest and meal breaks are provided in accordance with the Employment Relations Act 2000:

Duration of ShiftBreak Entitlement
Between 2 and 4 hours10 minute paid rest break
Between 4 and 6 hoursOne 10 minute paid rest break and one 30 minute unpaid meal break
Between 6 and 8 hoursTwo 10 minute paid rest breaks and one 30 minute unpaid meal break
More than 8 hoursAdditional breaks in line with the above entitlements for each additional 2 hours worked

While the Employer will endeavour to provide breaks at evenly spaced intervals, operational requirements may require flexibility. Employees must notify management immediately if they are unable to take their break entitlements. Requests to vary break timing must be submitted in writing.

5. Punctuality and Lateness

Employees must attend work at the times specified in their employment agreement. If an employee is going to be late, they must notify their manager as soon as possible and no later than their scheduled start time.

  • The correct procedure will be followed when requiring an employee to attend a disciplinary meeting

  • Action will only be taken after careful investigation and after the employee has had the opportunity to present their side

1. Purpose

This policy provides employees with a procedure for raising personal grievances and workplace complaints, with the aim of resolving issues fairly and promptly.

2. What is a Personal Grievance?

A personal grievance is a serious concern or dispute relating to:

  • Dismissal
  • Action taken by the Employer against an employee
  • Alleged discrimination
  • Alleged sexual harassment
  • Alleged racial harassment
  • Alleged duress in relation to union membership or non-membership

Less serious complaints should be raised with management in the first instance.

3. Employee Entitlements

Where an employee has a grievance, they are entitled to have it addressed through either an informal or formal resolution process. Although the Employer encourages informal resolution in the first instance, employees may elect to commence the formal process at any time.

4. Employee Responsibilities

Employees raising a grievance must:

  • Do so promptly and in accordance with this procedure
  • Maintain confidentiality, disclosing details only to those directly involved in the complaint or its resolution
  • Cooperate with any investigation and attend meetings as directed
5. Informal Resolution

Where possible and where the employee feels comfortable, grievances should be resolved informally by approaching the person or people involved directly. Employees may also ask management to informally address the matter with the relevant person.

Note: Informal resolution does not involve a formal investigation. If an investigation is required, the formal process must be followed.

6. Formal Resolution
6.1 Lodging a Formal Grievance

To lodge a formal personal grievance, the employee must submit full details in writing to management, together with any supporting evidence.

Time limits for raising a formal grievance:

Grievance TypeTime Limit
Sexual harassmentWithin 12 months of the event
All other grievance typesWithin 90 calendar days of the event

Grievances raised outside these timeframes will not be considered. If the grievance is against the employee’s direct manager, the grievance should be submitted to a more senior member of the team.

6.2 Investigation

Following receipt of a formal grievance, a meeting will be convened to obtain full particulars. The Employer will then investigate and consider all available facts before making a decision.

6.3 Outcome

The employee will be advised in writing of the decision. All decisions will be final.

7. Grievances Relating to Bullying and Harassment

Grievances relating to bullying and/or harassment will be dealt with in accordance with the Bullying and Harassment Prevention Policy (HR-POL-002).

8. Vexatious Grievances

Any grievance found to be vexatious is treated by the Employer as serious misconduct and may result in disciplinary action.

  • Information supporting the basis that the work is (or was) predominantly performed by female employees and is or has been undervalued

3. Resignation

All resignations must be provided in writing and must state the reason for the resignation. Notice periods will be as specified in the individual employment agreement. Employees are expected to work their full notice period unless otherwise agreed in writing.

4. Failure to Give Notice

If an employee terminates their employment without giving or working the required notice period, an amount equal to the additional cost of covering their duties during the unworked notice period will be deducted from any termination pay owing.

5. Return of Employer Property

Upon termination of employment, employees must return all Employer property in their possession on or before their last day of employment. Property to be returned includes, but is not limited to:

  • Computer equipment, mobile devices, and access credentials
  • Uniforms and personal protective equipment
  • Keys, access cards, and security passes
  • Documents, files, and confidential materials

Failure to return property within seven days of the last day of employment will result in the cost of the items being deducted from any payments outstanding.

6. Return of Vehicles

Any Employer and/or host business vehicle must be returned to Employer or host business premises immediately upon termination of employment. Failure to return the vehicle will result in the cost of its recovery being deducted from any payment outstanding.

7. Garden Leave

If either party serves notice to terminate employment, the Employer may require the employee to take garden leave for all or part of the remaining notice period. During garden leave, the employee will continue to receive full pay and contractual benefits but will not be required to attend the workplace.

8. Final Pay

Final pay will be processed in accordance with the Holidays Act 2003 and the employee’s employment agreement. This will include all outstanding wages, accrued but untaken annual leave, and any other entitlements.

9. Confidentiality Post-Employment

Obligations relating to confidentiality and non-disclosure of the Employer’s information continue after termination of employment. Former employees must not use or disclose any confidential information obtained during their employment.
