Sales Foundations & Philosophy

Everything you need to know before making a single call: what we believe, how the pipeline works, and how the day is structured.

What We Believe
  • Sales is leadership. We do not beg — we lead.
  • We solve real problems. If they do not have a problem, we move on.
  • We do not sell placements. We sell outcomes: less stress, faster hires, and tradies that actually show up.
  • We do not push — we diagnose. Then we prescribe the right solution.
  • We only work with serious buyers. We qualify hard and disqualify faster.
  • We follow up relentlessly — not to annoy, but to serve.
  • We do not sell on hope. We sell on proof, systems, and risk-free value.
  • We solve people's problems. We are the solution.
How We Sell
  • We interrupt patterns. No boring intros, no salesy garbage. We are real, direct, and disarming.
  • We focus on the client's pain first — not our features.
  • We book meetings, not sell on the cold call.
  • We use frameworks (like Rocket Selling) so every sales call follows a proven flow.
  • We are obsessed with speed: speed to contact, speed to call, speed to close.
  • We build trust by showing how we think — not just what we offer.
What Success Looks Like
  • The client says: “Finally, someone who gets it.”
  • They do not feel sold. They feel understood, helped, and confident.
  • Our deals move fast because we create urgency and eliminate risk.
  • The client sees us as an extension of their team — not a vendor.
Sales success is not about being the most charismatic person in the room. Sales success comes from consistently following a proven system.

Objective

To consistently generate and load targeted leads (Aussie trade-based businesses) into Apollo, ensuring they are filtered by industry, role, and decision-maker so outreach can begin with cold calls and email sequences.

Stage 2: Cold Outreach

Deliver the cold call opener and hook, then book a Discovery Call -- or trigger the follow-up email sequence.

Objective

To make first contact with decision-makers using a proven cold call opener and hook, then push to book a Discovery Call. If no answer, follow up using Apollo's Cold Outreach Email Sequence.

Responsible & Tools
Responsible
Salesperson
Tools Used
Apollo.io (lead tracking + email sequence), phone or VoIP, Outlook or Apollo Email, LinkedIn (optional warm touchpoints).
Cold Call Script — 10 Moves
1
Pattern Interrupt + Familiarity. Say their name like you know them: “Bob!” (pause — let their brain catch up.) “Les here.”
2
Take away their time objection first. “Mate, I've only got about 20 seconds before I jump into another meeting, so I'll keep this sharp.” This flips the power dynamic instantly.
3
Take away their “send me info” objection. “Before I flick you some info…” This shuts down the reflex “just email it to me.”
4
Take away their “not interested” objection. Do not pitch. Ask for help: “…I've got two quick questions I need your help with around your [trade]. Then I'll get out of your hair.”
5
Question 1 — diagnose their pain. “How are you finding good [trade] right now? Actually getting them on the tools — is that currently easy, or a bit of a nightmare?”
6
Question 2 — quantify the consequence. “And if you had the right guy starting next week — full-time, work-ready — what job or project would that let you attack properly?”
7
Deliver the value in one line. “I help Aussie companies solve that exact problem by tapping into a pool of ready-to-move Kiwi tradies that 99% of your competitors don't even know exist.” (Pause. Let it land.)
8
Micro-CTA — no hard ask. “I can send you 2-3 CVs to show you what the talent looks like. What roles are you actually desperate for right now?”
9
Book the call without begging. “Sweet — easiest way to do this is a quick 10-minute Teams chat so I know exactly who you need. Does Tuesday afternoon or Wednesday morning work?”
10
If they push back:
Price: “Fair — and honestly, staying short-staffed is costing you way more than any recruiter invoice. How long would it take you to get ROI on one good sparkie? A month?”
Stall: “Totally. What would happen if they said no?”
Vehicle: “100%. Plenty of good businesses have been burned by recruiters. If I landed you the exact guy you've been chasing, would that solve the problem or is there something deeper behind the hesitation?”
Full script -- short punchy version: “Bob! Les here. Mate I've only got 20 seconds before I jump into another meeting. Before I flick you some info, I've got two quick questions I need your help with around your sparkies. Then I'll get out of your hair. How are you finding good guys right now -- easy, or a nightmare? And if the right guy started next week, what job would that unlock for you? Sweet -- I've got Kiwi tradies ready to move that your competitors don't even know exist. I'll shoot you a couple CVs. Easiest way is a quick 10-minute Teams. Tuesday arvo or Wednesday morning?”

Hook delivered clearly and concisely
Discovery Call offered or booked
Objection handled correctly (if raised)
Call outcome logged in Apollo (Booked, No Answer, Rejected)
Cold Email Sequence triggered if no answer
Reminder set for agreed follow-ups

2.3

Stage 3: Presentation & Offer (Closer Script)

Run this script live on every Closer Call. Every conversation follows the same eight stages -- skipping a stage weakens the close.

Objective

To present the Risk-Free Tradie Placement System using the Rocket Selling framework — not to convince, but to show how our system solves their exact problem and let the offer close itself.

Responsible & Tools
Responsible
Salesperson
Tools Used
Microsoft Teams or Zoom (screen share if needed), Apollo.io (deal tracking + notes), the Closer Script.
Every sales conversation follows the same eight-stage structure. Skipping stages reduces effectiveness.
Stage 1 — Frame the Call

Purpose: lower resistance and position the conversation as a simple discovery discussion rather than a sales pitch. Most prospects expect pressure — this stage removes it.

Training note: deliver this calmly and casually. Do not sound rehearsed. Do not rush past the "All good?" -- wait for agreement before moving on.

Script:
“Pretty easy today, I just gotta see what's happening over there and see if we can actually help.”
“If we can I'll walk you through what we do and if I can't I'll tell you straight away — all good?”

Transition: once they say “all good” or any version of yes, move straight to Stage 2. Do not explain anything else yet.

Stage 2 — Clarify the Problem

Purpose: understand the client's staffing situation in detail -- what roles they need, how long they've been open, what they've tried, and the financial impact.

Training note: do not rush. Let the client talk. Dig deeper on short answers. Rule -- never move out of Stage 2 with incomplete information. A weak discovery produces a weak close every time.

Script:
“I've had a look into [Company Name] — I'm guessing it's [trade] you're after. What skills do you need in these guys?”
“How long have you been trying to fill these roles?”
“What have you already tried to fill these roles?”
“How is not having these [trade] affecting your business?”

Dig deeper — use: “In what way?” / “How do you mean?” / “What does that look like day to day?”

“Is it affecting you financially in any way?” or “Roughly what could that role generate for the business in a week?”

“And if you had these [trade] and a fully staffed team — what does that mean for your business, what does that look like?” — this is the most important question in discovery. Write the answer down word for word; you use it in Stage 5.

“How quickly do you want to get these roles filled — is this a priority?”

If the prospect goes quiet or vague: reflect it back and ask one more question (“When you say tricky — what does that look like day to day?”); normalise silence (“Take your time — I just want to make sure I actually understand what's going on before I say anything.”); make the money question easier (“Rough ballpark is fine.”); or check engagement directly (“Can I ask — is this something you're actually looking to solve right now, or is the timing not right?”).

Stage 3 — Label the Problem

Purpose: confirm the problem and impact. Get the client to agree with your summary — this reinforces the pain and creates alignment before presenting the solution.

Training note: keep the summary simple. Use their words. The client must confirm the summary before you move into the solution.

Script:
“Okay, let me make sure I've got this right. You're trying to find [trade] — you've been struggling for [time period], and everything you've tried so far — [Seek / ads / referrals etc] — hasn't solved it. Because of that you're currently losing about [$ per week]. And if this keeps going for another few months, it's going to cost the business roughly [$ amount]. Is that a fair summary?”

Stage 4 — Post-Label Question

Script: “Okay, based on that here's exactly how we'd solve it.”

Stage 5 — Sell the Vacation (The Outcome)

Purpose: shift the conversation from the problem to the result the client wants. People buy results, not services.

Training note: tailor this to their specific pain points from discovery. The script is only the skeleton -- always link the solution back to what they mentioned.

Script:
“When we get you this [trade] within 30 days — [their dream outcome in their exact words].” (Pause — let it land. Do not fill the silence.)
“And your business stops losing money.”
“So what makes us different from every other option you've already tried?” (pause)
“We open up a whole new talent pool — New Zealand. Qualified, ready to go and not being fought over by every other business in your area.”
“They've also made a decision to uproot their lives and move to Australia. Which means they show up with a different mindset. They want to give it a real crack.” (Pause)
“Do you think that could help your business?” (Wait for their answer.)
“From here the process is simple…”

Transition: whatever they say after “do you think that could help” — move straight into Stage 6. Do not linger.

Stage 6 — Explain the Process & Remove Risk

Purpose: remove uncertainty. Explain how the service works simply, then immediately introduce the guarantee to reduce perceived risk.

Training note: keep it simple. The client only needs to understand three key steps. Introduce the guarantee immediately after. They should leave thinking "this sounds easy and low risk."

Script:
“We send you CVs that match exactly what you need. You interview the ones you like and choose your preferred candidate. Once the contract is signed, we handle the relocation support so they hit the ground running. So that's how it works, easy and straightforward.” (pause half a beat)
“And here's how we remove the risk for you. If the hire doesn't work out in the first three months, we replace them. No drama. No extra cost. Does that make sense?”

Common objection at this stage — “What happens if the candidate leaves after 3 months?”:
“Great question — some of our best clients asked that before working with us. No one can guarantee someone won't change their mind. What we can control is who we put in front of you — we sit down with them properly before they start and just before the 3 month period to make sure they are committed long term. And if they aren't, we replace them immediately. But the bigger risk to your business isn't what happens after three months. It's what this role is costing you every week it stays unfilled. You mentioned earlier it's about [$X] a week. So if this drags on for another three months, that's roughly [$X × 12 weeks] gone. So you have lost ($$) in revenue and you're still in the same position.”

Stage 7 — Price / Payment Model

Purpose: clearly present the investment calmly and confidently.

Training note: state the price and stop talking. Do not apologise. Ask about the payment structure -- not whether the service is worth it. If they raise a price concern, redirect to the cost of the problem.

Script:
“In terms of investment its $16K. We split into two payments — one when the employment contract is signed, the second one month after they start. That keeps it easy on cash flow and lets you see a return quickly. Does that payment structure work for your business?”

Common objection — “That's expensive / more than I expected”:
“I totally understand — you want to make sure this is the right investment.” (Pause) “Let me ask you something. If we put price to the side for a second, do you think we can actually solve your staffing problem?” (If yes) “You mentioned earlier it's costing your business about [$X] a week not having this role filled. So over five weeks, that's roughly [$X x 5] gone — and you're still dealing with the same problem.” (Pause) “So the real risk isn't the $16K. It's doing nothing while the business keeps losing money every week. Fair?”

Stage 8 — Advance the Process (The Close)

Purpose: move the conversation from discussion to action.

Training note: do not turn this into a big sales close. Present the next step and stop talking. Silence is powerful here.

Script:
“The next step is for me to send through our Terms of Business and once you sign that we can get started.” (stop talking)
(If yes) “Perfect. I'll send our Terms of Business over for signing — and all I need from you is a detailed job description so we can get started right away.”

Referral (Bonus Step)

Purpose: generate additional opportunities from every sales conversation.

Training note: always ask if the conversation has been positive. Keep it simple and natural.

Script:
“Hey, just one quick question before you go. Who else do you know who's dealing with the same staffing issues that I may be able to help?” (Pause) (If they mention someone) “Perfect. Are you OK introducing us through email?”

Why Each Stage Matters (Training Notes)

The call script is the skeleton. The reasoning below is everything underneath it — understand it deeply enough that the conversation feels natural instead of memorised.

Frame the Call
The moment a business owner picks up the phone from a salesperson, their guard goes up. Telling them upfront this is a simple fact-finding conversation -- and that you'll tell them straight away if you can't help -- separates you from every other salesperson they've dealt with. The "all good?" is the first small agreement of the call; do not rush past it.
Clarify the Problem
This is the most important stage of the entire call. Do not rush from impact to money -- the client needs to fully feel the impact before you put a number on it. When they say the role costs them $4,000 a week and it has been empty for three months, they have just told themselves they have lost $48,000. You did not tell them that -- they worked it out themselves. That is far more powerful than any pitch.
The Dream Outcome Question
The single most important question in discovery. Whatever they say -- "scale the business", "stop turning down work", "get my weekends back" -- write it down word for word. You hand it straight back to them in Stage 5: "When we get you this [trade] within 30 days -- [their dream outcome, their exact words]." You are not selling them a hire. You are selling them back the exact future they just described.
Why the Pause Matters
After the outcome line in Stage 5, what happens in the silence is the prospect imagining the outcome -- let them. Do not fill it.

3.1

Price Objections

Never argue the price -- isolate it, then reconnect to the weekly cost of leaving the role unfilled.

Training Context

Price objections are rarely about money. They are about certainty. When the value of solving the problem is clear enough, the price becomes far less important.

Rule: never argue about the price. Shift the conversation back to the cost of leaving the problem unsolved.
Client Says: "That's expensive / more than I expected."

Script:
“I totally understand — you want to make sure this is the right investment.”

Isolate:
“Putting price aside for a moment… do you believe we can actually solve this staffing problem for you?” (Wait for yes)

Reconnect to cost:
“You mentioned earlier that not having this role filled is costing your business around [their number] per week, right?” “So if this role stays unfilled for another five weeks… that's around [$$] gone. And you're still in the exact same position.” (Pause) “So the real risk here isn't the $16k. The real risk is doing nothing, losing [$$] and still being in the exact same position.” (Pause and hold)

If they respond positively:
“Perfect. So just to recap the structure — the investment is $16,000, split into two payments. $8,000 when the candidate signs the employment contract and $8,000 one month after they start. Does that payment structure work for your business?”

If still hesitant — stack with the clarity question:
“Let me ask you something directly — what would it take for this to be a yes today?”

Email Reframe — Price Concern, Still Interested

Use when they replied something like: “$16K is a lot”, “I need to think about the cost”, “still trying to get over the line with the outlay.”

Hey [Name],

Fair enough — $16K is a real number and I get that.

Here's how I'd think about it: what's it costing you each week without this role filled? Lost jobs, stretched crew, turning work away — that's real money leaving the business.

The $16K covers the search, vetting, references, getting them over here, and a 90-day replacement guarantee if it doesn't work out. You're not paying for a CV — you're paying for a risk-managed hire with full work rights from day one.

Worth a quick call to run the numbers together?

3.2

Risk / Vehicle Objections

Reduce perceived risk with the replacement guarantee and pastoral care check-ins, without over-promising.

Training Context

This is rarely about the three-month period itself. The client is really saying: “I don't want to take a risk and lose money.”

Rule: do not try to guarantee perfection. Explain how your process reduces risk and bring the conversation back to the cost of the unfilled role.
Client Says: "What happens if they leave after three months?"

Script:
“Great question. Some of our best clients actually asked the exact same thing before they started working with us. And the honest answer is that no one can guarantee a person will never change their mind. But what we can control is who we put in front of you.”

“Something we do differently is we stay involved after the placement. We check in before that three-month mark to make sure they're looking to stay long term. And if they're not, we replace them.” (Pause)

If still hesitant:
“You mentioned earlier that not having this role filled is costing your business around [their number] per week, right? So if this role stays unfilled for another five weeks… that's around [$$] gone. And you're still in the exact same position.” (Pause) “So the real risk here isn't the $16k. The real risk is doing nothing, losing [$$] and still being in the exact same position.”

Email Reframes for Related Objections

Comparing to a 482 visa: A 482 involves sponsorship costs, migration agent fees, legal work, and months of admin before anyone sets foot on site, plus ongoing visa obligations. A New Zealand citizen has full Australian work rights from day one -- no visa, no sponsorship, no migration agent. The $16K covers everything: search, vetting, shortlisting, relocation coordination, references, and a 90-day replacement guarantee.

Guarantee concern -- what happens after 3 months: We only put forward candidates who are looking to stay long term. You interview them yourself before placement. We run a 12-week pastoral care program with regular check-ins, and sit down with you and the candidate together before the 3-month mark. If at any point during those 12 weeks you don't feel they're in it for the long haul, we replace them at no extra cost.

3.3

Stall Objections

Determine if the stall is genuine or a soft no, then use BAMFAM to keep the deal moving.

Training Context

This usually means either they genuinely need approval, or they are still uncertain about something. The goal is to quickly uncover which one.

Rule: do not immediately accept the stall. First determine whether the other person is truly a decision maker or whether the client is simply hesitant.
Client Says: "I need to talk to my partner / business partner."

Script:
“Totally fair. Makes sense you'd want to run something like this past them. Can I ask you something quickly? What would happen if they said no?” (Pause)

If they say they'd probably still do it: the decision is effectively made — move forward.

If they wouldn't move forward without their partner:
“No worries — can I ask you something, do they know you're struggling with this staffing issue at the moment? And they know how much it's affecting the business? So if there's a solution here that solves that problem… do you think they'd be opposed to you fixing it today?” (Pause)

If still hesitant: “Ok no worries. Just out of curiosity, what do you think their main concern would be?” — this draws out the real objection.

BAMFAM (Book A Meeting From A Meeting):
“No worries at all. When do you think you'll be speaking with them?” (Pause) “Perfect. Let's book a quick follow-up call after that so we can go through everything together. I'll send you a calendar invite after this.”

Email Reframes for Related Stalls

General Manager or partner needs to approve: offer a quick one-pager laying out what's included, how the process works, and how the fee compares to leaving the role open -- or offer a joint call.

Hiring a Sales Rep (SOP)

The exact 6-step hiring process for closers -- job ad, phone screen, Round 1 roleplay, shortlist, Round 2, and offer.

Process Overview

This SOP exists so anyone on the team can run the hiring process exactly the way Les would — same filter, same standard, same questions asked at every step. Follow it in order. Do not skip steps, send the objection sheet early, or soften the price pushback in Round 1.

StepWhat Happens
1. Job AdPost role on Seek + Trade Me
2. ApplicationCandidate applies, 1-minute phone screen, pass, send script + pre-call brief
3. Round 1Individual Zoom roleplay (3-5 min) -- price objection test
4. ShortlistScore against the criteria below, decide who advances
5. Round 2In-person, office -- full roleplay + Risk/Stall objections + fit chat
6. OfferJob offer conversation with finalist(s)
Step 1 -- Job Ad

Post the role on Seek and Trade Me. Keep the ad structured around commission-heavy, high-reward earning potential — low base, high commission — since that naturally filters toward people comfortable with performance-based pay.

Step 2 -- Application, Phone Screen, Send the Script

2A. The 1-minute phone screen (do this first, before anything else): whoever is on CVs rings every applicant for a quick, informal call. You are judging fluency and energy live -- not asking someone to confirm it. Ask where they are based (must be NZ-based), how soon they could start, and what made them apply for a sales role. Anyone who fails on fluent English, NZ location, realistic availability, or baseline energy does not get the script.

2B. Send the script + pre-call brief (same email, once the phone screen is passed): send the Client Call Script and confirm their Round 1 Zoom slot, plus a one-line brief that this will be a live roleplay, not a recital. Do NOT send the objection-handling sheet at this stage -- that stays back so Round 1 is a real test.

Step 3 -- Round 1: Individual Zoom Roleplay

Book each candidate an individual slot (never group -- it gets easier for whoever goes last). Total time per candidate: 3-5 minutes including feedback.

1
Vague opener. "Yeah look, it's been a bit of a nightmare mate, we're short-staffed." Testing: do they dig, or jump straight into pitching?
2
One more vague answer, then force the cut. After 2-3 exchanges, cut in: "So what can you actually do for me?" Testing: can they pivot into the pitch on a shorter runway, and hold the pause after the outcome line?
3
Cold price pushback (not in the script they were sent): "$16K feels pretty steep for a recruiter, mate." Testing: the single highest-signal moment in the whole test -- do they hold the price, or cave/discount/over-justify?
4
Neutral, non-committal response. "Yeah... nah, that's a fair bit to think about." Then go quiet. Testing: do they wait for permission, backpedal, or self-advance into the next step unprompted?
5
End it, give feedback out loud, on the spot. One strength, one thing to work on. Testing: how they take feedback -- defensive, blank, or "got it, noted."
Step 4 -- Shortlist Criteria
What to ScoreLook For
Discovery instinctDid they dig before pitching, or pitch blind?
Pause disciplineDid they let silence sit after the outcome line, or rush to fill it?
Price nerveDid they hold the price, or cave/discount/over-justify?
Self-advanceDid they move to next steps unprompted, or wait to be told?
Advance anyone who is solid on at least 3 of the 4. Weak on all four = no. Borderline calls go to Round 2 rather than being cut here.
Step 5 -- Round 2: In-Person, Full Roleplay

Finalists only, in the office (not Zoom) -- this is your chance to see how they show up in person. Run the full script live, start to finish, and now bring in the objection sheet for real: a Risk objection and a Stall objection on top of the price pushback. Afterward, drop out of character: why do they want this role, how do they feel about low base / high commission, and what does high-volume outbound calling look like for them day to day?

Step 6 -- Job Offer

If Round 2 confirms them, move straight to the job offer conversation -- no third round needed. By this point you have tested skill (Round 1), depth (Round 2 roleplay), and fit (Round 2 conversation).

Why this process works: the script gives every candidate a fair shape to prepare against. What you control live -- the vague answers, the shortened discovery window, the cold price objection -- cannot be memorised in advance. You are not hiring the person who best recites the script, you are hiring the person who can maneuver when the call does not go the way they rehearsed.

5.1

Training & Scaling (Hormozi Method)

How Stand Up trains and scales a sales team using Alex Hormozi's principles -- proof, system, hiring, and the 9-level ladder.

Founder Proves the Process First

Before hiring salespeople, the founder must prove the sales process personally. Salespeople cannot discover the process — they can only execute an already proven process. The founder must first determine who the ideal customer is, what problem they are trying to solve, what objections appear repeatedly, what messaging resonates most, what the offer must look like to convert, and what the closing structure should be.

Training rule: salespeople are not hired to "figure it out." They are hired to execute a proven process. Founder closes deals -> process is documented -> salespeople are trained to repeat it.
Turn the Founder's Brain into a System

Once the founder's sales approach works, convert it into a repeatable structure: call framework, question flow, problem diagnosis process, objection handling framework, offer presentation structure, and close process.

Key rule: if the sales process lives only in the founder's head, the business cannot scale. The sales system becomes the company's intellectual property.
Hiring, Training & Scaling
Hiring Salespeople
Hire for traits, not experience. Experience does not predict success. Traits that matter: work ethic, coachability, competitiveness, emotional resilience, hunger for success. People with these traits can be trained; people without them cannot.
Sales Training System
Training is built around real sales calls. Every call should be recorded. Identify the best calls, break down why they worked, and show new hires exactly what success looks like -- this shortens the learning curve dramatically.
Performance Scoreboards
Sales teams must operate like sports teams -- performance must be visible. Every salesperson should see calls made, meetings booked, deals closed, revenue generated. Leaderboards create competition; competition drives performance.
The 9-Level Sales Ladder
To retain top performers, promotions must happen frequently. Junior: Levels 1-3. Intermediate: Levels 4-6. Senior: Levels 7-9. Each promotion includes increased responsibility, compensation, and higher expectations. Employees should feel roughly six months from the next promotion.
Performance Management
Track performance weekly, coach underperformers quickly, set improvement targets, and remove persistent low performers. A team of high performers will outperform a larger team with weak members.
The Sales Scaling Formula
  1. Founder proves the sales process
  2. The process is documented and scripted
  3. Salespeople are hired for traits
  4. Training is built using real call recordings
  5. Performance scoreboards drive competition
  6. Promotions are structured using the nine-level ladder
  7. Underperformers are removed
This creates a predictable sales engine -- transforming sales from a founder-dependent activity into a repeatable system that any trained salesperson can execute.

Script Memorisation Method
  1. Print two copies of the script
  2. Read the full script out loud
  3. Black out one word with a marker
  4. Say the script again -- if they stuff up the blacked-out word, look at the clean copy and restart
  5. When they get it right, black out the next word
  6. Repeat until every word is blacked out
A 400-word script means they say it 400 times. By the end they don't think about the words -- they just say them. This is what allows natural delivery, real listening, and confident tone.

6.2

Instagram DM Sales Script

The message-by-message DM sequence for inbound leads -- from pain recognition through to close.

How to Use This Script

Inbound leads -- pain recognition through to close. Send one message, wait for their reply, react (heart/thumbs up), then send the next. Never stack messages. Replace anything in [brackets] with what they have actually told you -- their trade, their timeframe, their numbers, not generic ones.

1. Opener

“Hey, thanks for the message. Are you looking to hire staff, or are you looking for work?”

Wait for them to confirm they are hiring before continuing.

2. Clarify the Problem

Message 1 -- the role: “What roles are you struggling to fill at the moment?”

Message 2 -- duration: “How long have you been trying to fill this one?”

Message 3 -- the cost question: “Roughly, what could a fully qualified [trade] earn your business in a week?” This is the most important question in the sequence -- a number they say themselves lands far harder than one you hand them.

If they hesitate: “Fair enough -- so with them not in the seat, are you knocking back jobs, or just moving slower on the ones you've got?”

If they genuinely can't put a number on it: “No stress if that's hard to put a number on -- how many jobs would you say you've had to turn down or delay because you didn't have that [trade] in place?”

3. Label the Problem

“So you've been down a [trade] for [timeframe], that's roughly [$ figure] a week sitting on the table — and if that keeps going that's [monthly/annual figure] by the end of the year. Is that a fair summary?”

4. Sell the Vacation

Message 1: “If we get you a [trade] sorted in the next 30 days and that [$ figure] a week stops bleeding out of the business — what changes for you?”

Message 2 (after they answer): [echo their specific answer] — plus that [$ figure] a week isn't walking out the door anymore either. Sounds like a pretty different business in 30 days.

5. Priority Check

“How quickly do you want to get these roles filled? Is it a priority?”

6. Explain the Process

“Here's how it works — we don't fight over the same local candidates everyone else does. We tap into a talent pool in New Zealand, fully qualified, ready to go. Because they're moving countries for this, they come in with a different mindset. We send you CVs that match what you need, you interview and pick who you like, and once it's signed we handle the relocation side so they hit the ground running. Sound good?”

7. Remove the Risk

“Here's how we protect you — if the hire doesn't work out in the first 3 months for any reason, we replace them. No drama, no extra cost. Honestly though, it's not going to take you 3 months to know if someone's good — that's just there to protect you. Make sense?”

8. Price

“Alright — in terms of investment it's $16K, split into two payments: one when the contract's signed, the second one month after they start. Does that work for you?”

9. Advance the Process (The Close)

“Perfect, let's get the ball rolling. I'll send you an email with two things I need back from you — our Terms of Business to sign, and a quick job description form. Once I've got both of those back, we can get moving. What's the best email for you?”

Core Rules
  • 2-3 lines max per message -- if it reads like a paragraph, split it.
  • One question, then stop -- never stack two questions in one bubble.
  • React before you reply -- a heart or thumbs up on their message, then your next message.
  • Don't pitch until they've named the pain in their own words.
  • Every stage ends on something they have to respond to -- never send two messages in a row without getting their voice back in the conversation.
