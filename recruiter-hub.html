P0

Your Role as a Placer

Your job is to turn the candidates your sourcer books into placements. The sourcer finds candidates and books them in with you. From the internal interview onwards, everything is yours, all the way to a signed contract.

Speed matters. We've lost signed contracts by being too slow, so keep every candidate moving from one step to the next without waiting.

Two things matter most:

  1. Only put strong candidates in front of the client. The internal interview, reference checks and police check are where you decide that. A weak candidate wastes the client's time and costs us their trust.

  2. Keep things moving. Every step has a next action and a date. Never leave a candidate or a client waiting on you.

What you own:

  • Setting up new jobs: workflow, interview guide, job ad, and creating a task for your sourcer

  • Checking your calendar every morning and sending interview reminders

  • No-shows and rebooking

  • The internal interview and the 2-day cool off

  • Reference and police checks

  • Formatting the CV

  • Submitting the candidate to the client and booking the client interview

  • The client interview and debrief

  • The offer, the contract and the placement

What the sourcer owns:

  • Daily calls across the readiness folders

  • Screening calls

  • Booking candidates into your calendar

  • Passive sourcing: Bot 2, cold email and Trade Me JobSmart

P1

Job Setup

Every job starts with you. When a new job comes in, you set it up so you understand exactly what the client wants, and so your sourcer can start calling the next morning.

Tell: What You Need to Know

There are four steps: take the job, understand it, post the job ad, and create a task for your sourcer. Understanding the job should take 10 to 15 minutes. Don't rush it. If you don't understand the job, you can't judge whether a candidate is right for it.

Full Breakdown
Step 1: Take the job and assign your workflow

New jobs are emailed to recruiters when sales closes a client. When one comes in for your trades:

  1. Go to the JobAdder home page

  2. Find the new job under the No Applications stage

  3. Click the job to open it

  4. Go to the Details tab and click Edit

  5. Under Workflow, select your own workflow

  6. Click Save

Important: jobs come through without a workflow, so you have to assign it every time. If you skip this, the job won't track against you.

Step 2: Understand the job (10 to 15 minutes)

  1. Read the job description on the job card

  2. Go to Airtable and download the sales call transcript for the job

  3. Open Claude and run the SUR Tailored Interview Guide Generator prompt with the job description and the transcript. Save the output, because you'll use it in every internal interview for this job.

  4. Read the interview guide, especially the Client Intent Summary. It picks up things the client said on the sales call that aren't in the job description.

  5. Check that the job description, the transcript and the interview guide all match, especially on:

    • Pay rate and pay structure (hourly or salary, overtime, super)

    • Hours per week

    • Location

    • Accommodation, vehicle or other benefits

    • Must-have qualifications and experience

If anything doesn't match, especially pay, raise it with the sales team before the job goes to your sourcer.

Step 3: Create and post the job ad

  1. Open your Claude job ad thread

  2. Paste in the SUR Job Ad Prompt for Aus with the details from the job description

  3. Read the ad before posting. Never post anything you haven't checked.

  4. Name the ad Role | State, for example Electrician | Queensland

  5. In JobAdder: Actions → Post Job → paste the ad → select the platforms → Post

Platform When to post
JobRelay Always
Jora Always
JobSpace Always
JobAdder Board Always
Trade Jobs Always
Trade Me If needed

Check each platform to make sure the ad is showing correctly.

Step 4: Create a task for your sourcer

This is how your sourcer finds out about the new job.

  1. Click the job card

  2. In the top right corner, click the Action drop-down and select Create Task

  3. Add a task title, for example Review job

  4. Set the task date and priority

  5. Under Assignee, select your sourcer

  6. In the Activity drop-down, select Take Job Brief

  7. Click Create

Your sourcer gets a notification on the bell icon in the bottom left corner of JobAdder. They'll start calling from the next morning.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting to assign your workflow

  • Skimming the transcript, or skipping the interview guide

  • Creating the task for your sourcer when the pay doesn't match across the documents

  • Posting the job ad without reading it

  • Forgetting to create the task, or assigning it to the wrong sourcer

P2

Internal Interview

The internal interview is the most important conversation you have with a candidate before they meet the client. Get this right and everything after runs smoothly.

Tell: What You Need to Know

Your sourcer has already screened every candidate they book with you, so you don't need to re-check the basics. You go straight into selling the opportunity and digging deeper.

The interview is on Teams, or in person at our office if the candidate is in Auckland. The process is the same either way. Always use the interview guide you made for the job in P1. Never wing this one.

Full Breakdown
Every morning: check your calendar

  1. Open your calendar and check every internal interview booked for today

  2. Send the Meeting Day Reminder SMS to every candidate booked today

If a candidate doesn't show up

  1. Send the No-Show Interview Email and the No-Show Interview SMS, asking them to rebook

  2. Change their status to No Show-Up

  3. When they reply, rebook the interview yourself and change their status back to In Process

Before the interview

  • Read the candidate's profile, including the sourcer's call transcript in the Activity tab. Don't ask questions they've already answered.

  • Open the job description, with the selling points, must-haves and red flags ready

  • Open the interview guide you made in P1. Read the Client Intent Summary again.

  • Check JobAdder for client feedback from any candidate who failed the client interview for this job, and add those points to your questions

  • Have the Aus Video Interview Form open in JobAdder

Before your first interview, read "How to Use the Tailored Interview Guide Without Sounding Like a Checklist" in the Coaching Guide. It shows you how to weave the questions into a natural conversation.

Recording the interview

Every internal interview must be recorded. The recording is transcribed and summarised automatically, and the summary goes into the candidate's JobAdder profile under Activity → Notes. You don't need to take detailed notes. Stay focused on the candidate.

  1. Once the candidate has joined, click the three dots (...) in the Teams toolbar

  2. Select Start recording

  3. Teams tells everyone the call is being recorded. This is normal.

Important: start recording before you ask any questions. If you forget, you'll have to fill in the interview notes yourself. For in-person interviews, record on Teams using your laptop or phone.

Opening

"Hey [Name], good to meet you properly. Thanks for making the time. This is just a chat so I can give you a proper rundown of the role and see if it's a good fit for where you're at."

Part 0: Uncover the real why

This is the first real question in every interview. Les is firm on this: a surface answer is not good enough.

"What's making you want to make the move to Australia?"

Most candidates start with a surface answer, like better money or better weather. Ask 2 to 3 follow-up questions until you get the real reason:

  • "Sure, but what would that extra money actually let you do that you can't do right now?"

  • "Is there something specific driving that? A house deposit, supporting family back home, paying off debt?"

  • "Why does that matter to you at this point in your life?"

The real reason tells you whether they're genuinely motivated, and gives you something to come back to when you sell the opportunity and during the cool off.

Part 1: Run through the opportunity

Use the selling points from the job description. Make it exciting and relevant to this candidate. Cover the company, the duties, the pay, the type of work, growth, and lifestyle in Australia.

If accommodation or a vehicle is part of the offer, know whether it's temporary or permanent, how long it lasts, and what the candidate pays before you mention it. Don't oversell it. It comes back to bite you at the offer stage.

"From what you've told me, I think this could be a really good fit for you. It's not just a job, it's a genuine opportunity to step up, earn well, and set yourself up properly."

Part 2: Check they're keen

"Does that sound like something you'd want to hear more about?"

If they're hesitant, find out why before you go on.

Part 3: Screening questions

  • "If you were to move, what's your timeframe?"

  • "Would you be moving over on your own, or bringing family with you?"

  • "We don't need to know your criminal history, but is there anything serious that could stop you from moving to Australia for work?"

  • "Any medical conditions or restrictions we should be aware of that might affect site work?"

  • "Do you have a valid passport with at least 6 months validity?"

  • "Do you have any planned holidays or commitments in the next few months?"

If anything rules them out, close the call politely. If their timeframe has changed, update their Notice Period after the call. The folders will sort them.

Part 4: Financial readiness

Walk the candidate through the real costs, every time. This conversation stops dropouts after the offer.

Destination One-way flight from AKL Private room per week
Perth $650 $350
Adelaide $450 $300
Sydney $350 $500
Brisbane $350 $300
Melbourne $350 $350

Car: $4,000+. Total to be comfortable: around $5,000.

"Moving over costs more than most people expect. Your flight alone is $500 to $1,000. Accommodation runs $350 to $850 a week. Most people need a car within the first few weeks. So realistically, before your first pay comes in, you're looking at around $5,000 all up. Does that line up with where you're at financially?"

  • Clearly ready: "Perfect. We also offer a relocation package if you want it handled for you. I'll send you the info. Totally optional. Right, let's keep going."

  • Unsure: "I appreciate you being straight with me. Use the cool off period to sit down and work out the numbers. I'll send you a cost guide."

  • Clearly not ready: "Thanks for being honest. If the finances aren't there right now, it's going to be tough to make this work. Let's pause, you get yourself in a better position, and we reconnect when the timing's right."

Part 5: Work history and technical questions

"Talk me through your experience. What have you been working on recently? Walk me through the last role or two."

Listen for gaps against the must-haves. Then ask the 3 technical questions from your interview guide, plus any client feedback points for this job.

"What's the thing that's going to keep you going on the tough days out there?"

Part 6: Close

Wrap up with three things: the cool off, referees, and police check details.

Cool off: "Before we move you to the client interview stage, I want you to take the next 2 days to properly think this through. Relocating to Australia is a big decision. I'll send you an email with a summary of the role and the relocation information. We'll touch base in 2 days and decide together whether to move forward."

Referees: ask for 2 previous supervisors, with their name, company and phone number. Stay on the call until you have them. If the candidate says they'll send them later, let them know the details need to come straight to you.

Police check details: full name, email address, date of birth, gender, phone number, and home address.

Don't put the candidate forward to the client during the cool off. Wait for the Day 2 follow-up call.

After the interview

  1. Complete the Aus Video Interview Form in JobAdder

  2. Send the Cool Off Period Email, with the Relocation Cost Guide and the Trans-Tasman Bridge one-pager for the candidate's destination state

  3. Send the Document Request Email, asking for their driver's licence, passport and trade certificate

  4. Decide whether you rate the candidate. If you do, start the reference and police checks the same day (P3). If you don't, set Unsuccessful - Internal on the job card. Never click Reject.

  5. Book the Day 2 follow-up call

The Day 2 follow-up call

This is a real checkpoint, not a formality. Cover:

  • Financial readiness, with actual numbers

  • Whether they're happy to start within 4 to 6 weeks

  • Whether they're still happy with the location

  • Whether they've genuinely talked it through with their partner and family

  • Whether this move makes sense for where they want to be in a few years

Then:

  • Keen and clear on everything: move on to the CV (P4)

  • Hesitant: give them 2 more days. Don't push for a decision on this call.

  • Cold or wants to pull out: let them go gracefully and update their status

  • Has a concern the client could answer: tell them to raise it at the client interview, and remind them there's a second cool off once the offer comes through

See the Coaching Guide for the full Day 2 call script.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the morning calendar check and reminder texts

  • Re-asking questions the sourcer already covered. Read the transcript first.

  • Accepting a surface answer for why they want to move

  • Walking in without the interview guide and any client feedback

  • Forgetting to record

  • Ending the call without referees and police check details

  • Putting the candidate forward before the 2-day cool off is up

  • Clicking Reject on the job card

P3

Reference and Police Checks

After the internal interview, decide whether you rate the candidate. If you do, run two checks: references and a police check. Both must be clear before the candidate goes to the client.

Tell: What You Need to Know

Start both the same day as the interview, while the candidate is in their 2-day cool off. Don't wait for the cool off to end. Every day you wait is a day the candidate could change their mind or take another job.

If you don't rate the candidate, don't run the checks.

Full Breakdown
Part 1: Reference checks

You need at least 2 reference checks, both from supervisors the candidate gave you in the internal interview.

When to call. Don't call one referee and wait. Work through them together:

When What to do
Morning Call every referee on your list
Afternoon Call back anyone who didn't answer in the morning
After the 2nd missed call Send the Reference No Answer SMS and wait for a reply

The reference check script. Keep it casual. You want the referee to open up.

  1. Open. Say their first name, confidently, not as a question: "[Referee First Name]." When they answer: "Hey, it's [Your Name]. [Candidate Full Name] gave me your number, said it's okay to run a bit of a reference check." If they want more context, keep it short and keep going.

  2. The rundown. "Can you give me a full rundown please? The good, the bad, and the ugly." Then stop talking and let them go. Listen for their attitude on the tools, with the team and under pressure, for reliability and turning up on time, and for anything they mention without being asked. That's usually the most honest part. If they're short: "Yeah? What was that like day to day?" or "Anything stand out, good or bad?"

  3. Skills and experience. "What kind of [trade] work were they actually doing for you?" / "Were they good at it?" / "Any muck-ups or mistakes worth mentioning?" If it sounds too perfect: "Genuinely no issues at all?"

  4. If they're hesitant. If they seem cagey or want to involve HR, don't push. Note it as a flag. "No worries at all, appreciate you letting me know. If you think of anything after we hang up, feel free to flag it. Thanks for your time."

  5. The close. "Hey [Referee Name], I'm pretty much looking for an excuse not to hire [Candidate First Name], because you know a bad hire costs a lot of money and time, right? Can you give me any reasons not to hire [him/her]?" Then follow up with: "Would you rehire [him/her]?" Whatever comes next, silence, hesitation or a flat yes, that's your answer. Don't fill the silence.

What to do with the result:

Result What to do
1 red flag or lukewarm answer Ask the candidate for one more referee. Don't tell them why.
2 or more red flags out of 3 Don't progress them. Set Unsuccessful - Internal on the job card and let the candidate know we won't be moving forward.
All clear Carry on to the police check result and the CV (P4)

Log every reference check in JobAdder: the referee's name and company, what they said, and what you made of it.

Part 2: Police check

Submit the police check through the Airtable form on the same day as the internal interview: [Insert Airtable form link]

You need six details, which you collected at the end of the interview:

  • Full name

  • Email address

  • Date of birth

  • Gender

  • Phone number

  • Home address

Important: all six are required. An incomplete form won't be processed.

Results usually come back in 24 to 72 hours.

  • Clear: carry on to the CV (P4)

  • Serious flag: don't progress them. Set Unsuccessful - Internal on the job card and let the candidate know we won't be moving forward.

Never click Reject on the job card.

Checklist

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting for the cool off to end before starting the checks

  • Finishing one check before starting the other. Run them together.

  • Calling one referee and waiting, instead of calling them all in the morning

  • Talking over the silence after the close question

  • Submitting the police check with a detail missing

  • Not logging each reference in JobAdder

  • Clicking Reject on the job card

P4

CV Formatting

A well-formatted CV is what gets the client to say yes, so do it properly every time.

Tell: What You Need to Know

You have your own Claude thread for formatting CVs. You set it up once and use it for every CV after that. Claude remembers the SUR template and the rules in that thread, so you paste in the raw CV and the job details, and it does the formatting. You then check it, download it as a Word document, and upload it to JobAdder.

Two rules never change: first name only, never the full name, and no contact details. This protects our business.

Full Breakdown
One-time thread setup

  1. Open Claude at claude.ai and start a new conversation

  2. Type the setup message below and attach the SUR Formatted CV Template Word document:

"You are a CV formatting assistant for Stand Up Recruitment Ltd. I have attached our SUR Formatted CV Template. Please use this exact template structure for every CV I ask you to format in this thread. Rules to always follow: use the candidate's first name only, never their full name. Do not include any contact details. Always follow the SUR Formatted CV Template structure exactly. Follow the SUR Master CV Formatting Prompt rules for every CV. Format the output so I can download it as a Word document. Please confirm you have the template and are ready to format CVs."

  1. Once Claude confirms, bookmark the thread. This is where you format every CV from now on.

Important: if you start a new thread, Claude loses the template. Always go back to the same thread.

Formatting a CV, every time

  1. Have the candidate's raw CV and the job description ready, especially the client's specific ask and must-haves

  2. In your Claude thread, paste the SUR CV Formatting Prompt with the role details and the raw CV

  3. You can also upload the candidate's internal interview transcript. Only use the parts that support the client's specific ask. Don't add everything from the transcript.

  4. Check the CV before you download it:

  5. Download it as a Word document and upload it to the candidate's profile in JobAdder

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Starting a new Claude thread for each CV

  • Leaving in the candidate's full name or contact details

  • Adding everything from the interview transcript instead of only what the client cares about

  • Skipping the check before downloading. Claude does the heavy lifting, but you're responsible for what goes to the client.

  • Uploading to JobAdder without checking first

P5

Client Submission

The formatted CV is ready. Now get it in front of the client and get the interview booked as fast as possible.

Tell: What You Need to Know

Client submission has three parts: send the CV with a strong endorsement, give the candidate a heads-up, and follow up by phone until the interview is booked. Our aim is simple: get candidates in front of the client straight away.

We follow up by calling, not emailing. A call is harder to ignore than an email, and you can book the interview on the spot instead of waiting for a reply. Emails only go out when a call isn't answered.

Full Breakdown
Step 1: Send the endorsement email (Day 0)

Use the Endorse Candidate + Suggest Meeting email template.

  • Attach the formatted CV

  • Include the 2-sentence client submission blurb from the CV formatting (P4)

  • Pick an interview day and end the email with: "Will [Day] [Date] morning or afternoon work for you?"

  • Change the candidate's status in JobAdder to "Submitted to Client"

Important: always write the day and date in the client's timezone.

Step 2: Send the candidate a heads-up (Day 0)

Straight after sending the endorsement email, text the candidate using the Candidate Heads-Up SMS. Tell them the interview day, and ask them to keep both the morning and afternoon free while you wait for the client to confirm the exact time.

Important: convert the morning and afternoon to NZ time. NZ is 2 to 5 hours ahead of Australia, depending on the state and daylight saving, so a Perth morning can be a NZ afternoon.

Once the client confirms the exact time, go to P6 and book the interview.

Step 3: Call 1 (24 hours after the CV, no reply)

Call the client using the CV Follow-Up Phone Call Script. Check they've seen the CV and book the interview on the call.

  • Answered and keen: lock in the exact time before you hang up, then book it in JobAdder (P6). No email needed, because JobAdder sends the invite.

  • Answered but vague: ask if they're still actively hiring for the role. If it's not a priority, let them know you'll put the candidate forward to another client.

  • No answer: send Email 1: Just Tried Calling straight away.

If the CV went out on a Friday, make this call on Monday.

Step 4: Call 2 (48 hours after Call 1, still not booked)

Call the client again. Same goal: book the interview on the call.

  • Answered: same as Call 1. Lock in the time, or ask if they're still actively hiring.

  • No answer: send Email 2: Still Need a [Role Title]? straight away. It lets the client know that if hiring isn't a priority right now, you'll put the candidate forward to another client.

Step 5: No reply after Email 2

Don't wait. If you have other clients the candidate suits, put them forward straight away. Log the outcome in JobAdder.

If the interview day has passed

If the day you suggested in the endorsement email has already passed by the time you reach the client, offer a new day and send the Candidate Heads-Up SMS again, so the candidate keeps the new day free.

If the candidate has been turned down before

Use the Endorse Candidate: 2nd Attempt template instead of the normal endorsement email. It acknowledges the client's earlier feedback and shows you've acted on it.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting to text the candidate after sending the endorsement email

  • Writing the interview day in NZ time for the client, or in Australian time for the candidate

  • Following up by email instead of calling. Emails only go out after a missed call.

  • Hanging up without trying to book the interview

  • Accepting a vague maybe from the client. Push for a time or a clear no.

  • Holding a candidate for a client who isn't responding when another client could meet them now

  • Forgetting to update the candidate's status in JobAdder

P6

Client Interview

This is where your work pays off. The candidate meets the client, and you make sure it goes well.

Tell: What You Need to Know

There are three parts: getting the interview booked, sitting in on it, and finding out the result. You're not a bystander in the client interview. You're there to make sure the conversation goes the right way.

One rule protects the business: always book the client interview as a Contact Candidate Meeting in JobAdder. This sends the client and the candidate separate invites, so neither sees the other's contact details. Never put both on the same invite.

Full Breakdown
Part 1: Before the interview

  1. When the client confirms the exact time, text or call the candidate to make sure they can make it before you book anything

  2. Move the candidate to the Reference stage on the job card

  3. Book the interview in JobAdder:

    • Hover over the candidate's name and click the three dots beside it

    • Select Schedule Event, then Interview

    • Fill in the Event date, Start time and End time

    • Under "Looks like you are scheduling a...", select Contact Candidate Meeting

    • Click Save Event

  4. Text the client to let them know the invite has been sent

  5. Send the candidate the Candidate Prep Email, with the meeting link, the client contact's name, and the top 3 things the client wants from the job description

  6. Send the Candidate Prep SMS, a shorter version of the same thing

When you click Save Event, JobAdder automatically sends two separate emails, one to the client and one to the candidate. You don't need to send an invite yourself.

Part 2: During the interview

You sit in on every client interview. Your job isn't to run it. It's to introduce the candidate, follow how it's going, and step in when it needs a nudge.

Opening: "[Client name], this is [Candidate first name]. [Candidate], this is [Client name] from [Company]. I'll let you two get into it. I'm just here to help the conversation along if needed."

Stepping in:

  • If the candidate is struggling: "[Candidate], you mentioned to me that you can do [x, y, z]. Can you walk [Client] through how you approach that on site?"

  • If the client needs more: "[Candidate], [Client] is looking for someone who will commit to at least 24 months in this role. Is that something you see yourself doing?"

Your goal is to set things up for a yes, by guiding the conversation without taking it over.

Part 3: After the interview

Ask the candidate to drop off the call: "Thanks [Candidate], we'll be in touch very soon. I'm just going to have a quick chat with [Client] now."

Then debrief with the client: "What did you think? Does [Candidate] tick the boxes for you?"

If the client says yes:

  1. Confirm a possible start date with the candidate. Allow for the 2-day offer cool off and the time they need to move.

  2. Straight after the debrief, send the client the Post-Client Interview Email with Candidate Details. It gives them the candidate's full name and possible start date for the offer letter, and asks them to add the temporary accommodation and vehicle arrangements to the offer letter.

  3. Ask when they can send the offer letter

  4. Update the candidate's status in JobAdder

  5. Call the candidate to let them know they passed, and that you'll be in touch once the offer letter comes through

If the client says no:

  1. Ask for specific feedback: "Just so I can improve the next batch, what was missing for you?"

  2. Log the client's feedback on the job in JobAdder. It becomes part of the screening for every new candidate for this job.

  3. Set Unsuccessful - Client on the job card. Never click Reject. The candidate stays in whichever folder matches their availability, and you decide what to do with them next.

  4. Send the candidate a rejection email. Use Claude to write it, including the client's actual feedback.

  5. For your next candidate for this job, use the Endorse Candidate: 2nd Attempt template (P5)

If the client goes quiet

Every conversation ends with a date, never "I'll get back to you." Before you hang up from the debrief, always ask: "When can we expect a decision by?" If that date passes, follow the contract follow-up cadence (P8).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Putting the client and candidate on the same invite. Always use Contact Candidate Meeting.

  • Sitting back during the interview instead of stepping in when it's needed

  • Forgetting to send the Post-Client Interview Email after a yes

  • Not asking for specific feedback after a no, or not logging it for the next candidates

  • Clicking Reject on the job card

  • Ending the debrief without a date for the decision

  • Sending a generic rejection email instead of one with the client's actual feedback

P7

Offer

This is the finish line: get the signed contract back and place the candidate in JobAdder.

Tell: What You Need to Know

When the client sends the offer letter, you manage everything from there. You check the offer, let the client know you've got it, send it to the candidate through DocuSeal, give the candidate a 2-day cool off, run the Welcome & Relocation Chat, and place the candidate in JobAdder.

Your part ends when you place the candidate. That automatically starts onboarding and sends the handover emails to the client and the candidate. From then on, the admin team takes over.

Full Breakdown
Step 1: Check the offer letter

Before you do anything else, run the Offer Letter Review Prompt with the offer letter and the job description.

It always tells you first whether the candidate is paid hourly or by salary, and if it's a salary, what that works out to per hour. Then it checks for:

  • Hard stops: casual employment, no guaranteed hours, no notice during probation, a salary with no paid overtime, or a pay type that isn't clear

  • Things to discuss with the client: pay that doesn't match the job description, super included in the rate, a change from hourly to salary, promised benefits missing, clauses that could move the candidate to other sites, harsh probation terms, and deductions nobody mentioned

What to do with the result:

  • Hard stop: stop. Raise it with the client before the candidate sees anything.

  • Discuss with client: go through each one. Items marked [Recommended] should usually be raised. Items marked [Optional: your call] are up to you, and are often standard wording that may never be used.

  • Offer letter looks incomplete: get the full document before going any further

  • All clear: go to Step 2

Why the pay check matters: with a salary and no paid overtime, the candidate isn't paid for any hours past their normal weekly hours, even if they work them. We've had a candidate sign one of these. Catching it here protects our candidates.

Step 2: Let the client know you've got it

Reply with the Offer Letter Received Reply template on the same day the offer letter arrives. Tell them you'll get it to the candidate that day and follow up once it's signed.

Step 3: Send the offer through DocuSeal

  1. Log into DocuSeal

  2. Click Create to start a new document

  3. Name it [Your Name] - contract for candidate

  4. Click Upload and select the offer letter from the client

  5. Add a Signature Field for the candidate

  6. Click Send

  7. Go to the Detailed tab

  8. Fill in: Candidate Name, Name of the Company, Location, Start Date, Client First Name

  9. In the email fields, add the candidate's email and the client's email

  10. Untick "Send Email". The automation team's setup needs this to work properly.

  11. Click Add Recipients

Once you click Add Recipients, DocuSeal automatically emails the candidate the offer with a link to sign.

Important: the candidate can sign straight away. That doesn't replace the call in Step 4. Every candidate gets the call, whether they've signed or not.

Step 4: Call the candidate and book the Welcome & Relocation Chat

As soon as the offer has gone through DocuSeal, call the candidate. This call does three things: tells them the offer has arrived, explains the 2-day cool off, and books the Welcome & Relocation Chat for 2 days' time.

  1. Share the good news. "Hey [Name], it's [Your Name] from Stand Up. Good news, we've got your letter of offer through from [Client]. This is the big one. This means [Client] wants to bring you on board in Australia."

  2. Explain the cool off. "Now before anything gets signed, we give you a 48-hour cool-down period. That's non-negotiable on our end, and it's there for you. It's your time to sit with the contract properly, talk to your partner or family, and be honest with yourself about timing, location, and what this move actually means for your life. We'd rather you take those 48 hours and be certain than rush it and regret it."

  3. Explain what's in the email. "You'll get an email from us in the next few minutes with three things in it: your letter of offer from [Client], with the pay, start date, all of it. Our terms of business, which are standard Stand Up terms. And a relocation guide. Have a proper read of that one, because it covers your bank details, your tax file number, and all the admin you'll need sorted. In 48 hours you'll be on a call with me and someone from our pastoral care team, going through that guide step by step, so if you've already read it, the call moves a lot faster."

  4. Book the Welcome & Relocation Chat. "So let's lock that in now. We're talking [today], so 48 hours takes us to [day]. What time suits you on [day]? Great, I'll send you a Teams invite for [day] at [time]. That's you, me, and our pastoral care team."

  5. End on a high. "[Name], before I let you go, we're genuinely excited to have you on board. This is a massive step and we can't wait to see you thrive over there. Talk soon!"

Once it's booked, send the Welcome & Relocation Chat Email to confirm the date, time and Teams link.

Step 5: The Welcome & Relocation Chat

A video call with you, the candidate and the pastoral care team, 2 days after the offer goes out. Every candidate has this call, whether they've signed or not.

The call is to talk through the candidate's move: their finances, their start date and their location, with the team who'll help them through it. You also go through the Relocation Guide together. If something comes up that's a dealbreaker, let the client know and work out what to do next.

Step 6: Follow up until the signed contract comes back

Don't just wait. If the signed contract hasn't come back in a reasonable time, call or message. If the client is holding things up, follow the contract follow-up cadence (P8).

Step 7: Place the candidate in JobAdder

As soon as you have the signed contract and ID, place the candidate. This starts onboarding and sends the handover emails to the client and the candidate automatically.

  1. Go to the candidate's profile in JobAdder

  2. Click the three dots menu

  3. Select Place Candidate

  4. Fill in these four fields:

Start date: the date the candidate and client agreed.

Total fee: SUR charges either a flat fee, or 15% of the candidate's full yearly pay including super. Check the job description for which one applies and for the client's super rate. Super rates differ between clients, so never assume. If the rate isn't in the job description, ask the client before placing.

Fee type Formula
Flat fee $16,000 AUD
15%, hourly rate Hourly rate × weekly hours × 52 × (1 + super rate) × 15%
15%, yearly salary, super paid on top Yearly salary × (1 + super rate) × 15%
15%, yearly salary, super already included Yearly salary × 15%

To use the super rate: 12% super means × 1.12. 11% super means × 1.11.

Example: $45 an hour, 40 hours a week, 12% super. $45 × 40 × 52 = $93,600. Then × 1.12 = $104,832. Then × 15% = $15,724.80. At 11% super, the same job comes to $93,600 × 1.11 × 15% = $15,584.40.

Application status: pick the relocation option: Full relocation package (East Coast), Full relocation package (SA), Full relocation package (WA), or No relocation package.

If you're placing someone to replace a candidate who didn't work out in the role, select Replacement instead. In the Replacement field, type the full name of the person being replaced, exactly as it shows in JobAdder.

Signed offer: upload the contract signed by the candidate.

  1. Check all four fields, then click Create

  • Forgetting to call the candidate straight after sending the offer

  • Skipping the Welcome & Relocation Chat because the candidate has already signed

P8

Contract Follow-Up

Every conversation ends with a date. Not "I'll get back to you", but a specific date for a decision, a contract, or the next step.

Tell: What You Need to Know

Use this whenever a client goes quiet, whether you're waiting on a decision after the client interview or a signed contract after the offer has gone out. The same steps apply to both.

The main rule: every conversation ends with a specific date. If the client wants to think about it, your last question before you hang up is always: "When can we expect a decision by?"

The follow-up isn't a fixed ladder. It's one rule you repeat until the job is sorted or you hand it to Les.

Full Breakdown
The follow-up steps
When What to do
The date passes with nothing received Next business day: send Email 1
No reply to Email 1 Next business day: Call 1
Call 1 gives you a new date, and that one is missed too Next business day: send Email 2
No reply to Email 2 Next business day: Call 2, then stop and pass it to Les

Every time the client gives you a new date, start again from Email 1. Don't skip straight to a call. If the next step falls on a Saturday or Sunday, do it on Monday.

Example
Day What happens
Mon Interview happens. Client says: "We'll have a decision by Tuesday."
Tue Decision due. Nothing received.
Wed Email 1: check-in
Thu No reply, so Call 1. Client says: "You'll have the contract by Friday."
Fri Contract due. Nothing received.
Mon (Friday rolls over the weekend) Email 2: final email
Tue No reply, so Call 2. Stop and pass it to Les.
Email templates

  • Email 1, check-in (the business day after a missed date): use the Contract Follow-Up: Check-In template

  • Email 2, final email (when a second date is also missed and Email 1 got no reply): use the Contract Follow-Up: Urgency template

What to say on the calls

Call 1, after Email 1 gets no reply: "Hey [Name], just following up. Where are we at with the [contract/decision] for [Candidate]? Anything you need from us to get it moving?" Always get a new date before you hang up.

Call 2, after Email 2 gets no reply: "Hey [Name], wanted to get a clear answer today. Are we able to get this over the line, or do we need to have a different conversation about timing?" After this call, whatever happens, pass it to Les. Don't send any more emails or make any more calls until Les decides what's next.

How to pass it to Les

  • Usually: raise it at the next morning meeting with Les. The EA will follow it up with Les during the day and let you know what's next.

  • If it's urgent (the candidate is about to walk away, or the client finally replies partway through): don't wait for the meeting. Email Les straight away.

Keeping the candidate warm

Once Email 1 has gone out, give the candidate a quick call so they don't lose interest or take another job while you're waiting on the client.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Ending the debrief without a date

  • Sending Email 1 on the same day the date is missed. Wait until the next business day.

  • Skipping steps. Always email, then call.

  • Letting a step slip past the weekend. Do it on Monday, don't skip it.

  • Contacting the client again after Call 2. Stop and pass it to Les.

S0

Your Role as a Sourcer

Your job is to find New Zealand tradespeople who are keen to move to Australia and get them in front of a placer as fast as possible. Speed matters. Good candidates go elsewhere when we're slow.

You focus on calls. The placer handles everything from the internal interview onwards, so your day is spent on the phone, not on admin.

Two things matter most:

  1. Book Ready to go and Hot List candidates into internal interviews with the placer. That's the number one measure of a good day.

  2. Keep candidate data accurate. After every call, update Status, Notice Period, Location and Ideal Salary. The readiness folders and daily alerts only work if this data is right.

What you own:

  • Picking up new jobs the placer assigns to you

  • Your daily calling routine across the readiness folders

  • Screening calls

  • Booking internal interviews into the placer's calendar

  • Passive sourcing: submitting the Airtable form for Bot 2 and acting on its notifications, cold email, and Trade Me JobSmart

Calls come first. Screening calls and booking interviews are always your priority. Bot 2, cold email and JobSmart are passive sourcing: they bring candidates in, but they don't book anyone. Fit them around your calls, never in place of them.

What the placer owns:

  • Job setup and the job ad

  • Interview reminders, no-shows and rebooking

  • The internal interview

  • Reference and police checks

  • The CV, the client, the offer and the placement

S2

Receiving a New Job

When a new job comes in, the placer sets it up (workflow, interview guide and job ad), then creates a task in JobAdder assigning the job to you. You'll get a notification on the bell icon in the bottom left corner of JobAdder.

Tell: What You Need to Know

There's no separate short brief for you. The full job brief is on the job card, and everything you need is in it:

  • Job title, for example Excavator Operator

  • Pay rate, for example $55 an hour

  • Location, for example Melbourne

  • Must-have qualifications, for example a current ticket or years of experience

Read the job brief properly before you make a single call. You need a good understanding of the job: what the work involves, what the client must have, and what would rule a candidate out. You are the first filter. If you don't understand the job, you'll book the wrong people in front of the placer, and that wastes everyone's time, including the candidate's.

Once you've picked up the task, the job becomes part of your daily calling routine from the next morning.

Full Breakdown

  1. Click the bell icon in the bottom left corner of JobAdder and open the new task from the placer

  2. Open the job card linked to the task

  3. Read the full job brief from top to bottom. Don't skim it.

  4. Note the job title, pay rate, location and must-have qualifications. These are your filter on every call.

  5. Add the trade to your daily routine (S3) so you're filtering for it in every folder

  6. Start calling

If anything in the job brief is unclear or missing, ask the placer before you start calling. Don't guess what the client wants.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skimming the job brief, or skipping straight to the job title

  • Calling candidates before you understand the must-haves

  • Waiting for a separate brief. The job brief on the job card is all you need.

  • Not adding the new trade to your daily routine

Your Daily Routine

Every day, you follow the same routine.

Tell: What You Need to Know

First, you call everyone on your daily alert email. It arrives at 7AM every day from the automation team, and it lists the candidates you need to call that day.

Then you work through the four readiness folders in the same order every time. Ready to go and Hot List come first, because those are the candidates you can book with a placer. Warm List and Cold List come after, to find anyone who's become ready and to keep everyone's availability date up to date.

Full Breakdown

Step 1: Call everyone on your daily alert email. It arrives at 7AM. Call every candidate on the list before you start on the folders. On every one of these calls:

  1. Always get the four details: when they can move (Notice Period), where in Australia they want to go (Location), and their Ideal Salary. Then set their Status.

  2. If they can move within 3 months (the Ready to go or Hot List timeline), carry on into a full screening call (S6) and book them with the placer (S8).

  3. If it's longer than 3 months, update the four fields and move on. The folders will sort them automatically.

Step 2: Open your first folder.

  1. In JobAdder, hover over the Home icon

  2. Select Folders from the drop-down

  3. Select the folder you're working on. Always start with Ready to go.

Step 3: Check your columns. Make sure these four columns are visible. They're the fields you update after every call:

  • Status

  • Notice Period (this is the candidate's availability date)

  • Location

  • Ideal Salary

Step 4: Sort by newest. Set the Created column to sort descending, so the newest candidates appear at the top.

Step 5: Set your filters.

  • Categories: the trade you're sourcing for

  • Status: New, Unanswered, Unresponsive, No Engage and No Show-Up

Call the New candidates first. They've just come in and they're the hottest leads.

For No Show-Up candidates, read their notes before you call. The placer will already have sent them a No-Show email and text, and may have rebooked them.

Step 6: Work the folders in this order. Repeat Steps 2 to 5 for each folder.

Order Folder What you're doing
1 Ready to go Screen and book qualified candidates with the placer
2 Hot List Screen and book qualified candidates with the placer
3 Warm List Check if anyone is now ready. If not, still talk to them to confirm when they can move, and update their Notice Period.
4 Cold List Same as Warm List

Step 7: After every call, update the required fields (see S7).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Starting on the folders before calling everyone on the daily alert email

  • Starting with Warm or Cold List. Ready to go and Hot List always come first.

  • Skipping the column check, then forgetting to update a field

  • Not sorting by Created, and missing the newest candidates

  • Calling a No Show-Up candidate without reading their notes first

  • Hanging up on a Warm or Cold List candidate without updating their Notice Period

S6

Screening Call

The screening call is a short call to check whether a candidate is right for the job, and ready to move soon enough to book. You're checking the basics: their trade, their experience, their qualifications, when they can move, and whether they're a NZ citizen. It's not a deep interview. The placer does that.

Tell: What You Need to Know

Every screening call ends one of three ways:

  1. Qualified and can move within 3 months: book them with the placer before you hang up (S8)

  2. Qualified but can't move within 3 months: update their details and move on. The folders will sort them.

  3. Not suitable: close the call politely and update their status

The internal interview with the placer is a video call on Teams. If the candidate is in Auckland, it can be in person at our office instead.

Full Breakdown
Before the call

  • Have the candidate's JobAdder profile open

  • Read their CV. Know their trade and experience before you dial.

  • Have the job brief open, so you know the must-haves

Making the call

You don't dial candidates yourself. A bot connects the call, so it can be recorded and transcribed automatically.

  1. Open the candidate's profile in JobAdder

  2. Hover over the status section below the candidate's name

  3. Select Screening

  4. Your phone will ring. Pick it up and press any key.

  5. The bot then calls the candidate and connects you

Because the call is recorded and transcribed, you don't need to take notes. Focus on the conversation.

The script

Opening: "Hey [Name]! It's [Your Name] from Stand Up Recruitment. You showed interest in work in Australia, is that right?"

  1. Why move: "What's making you want to make the move to Australia?"

  2. Skills and experience: "I can see that you are a [trade]. Can you expand a bit more on your skills and experience?"

  3. Qualifications: "Are you qualified in that trade?" / "Do you hold a current [specific ticket]?"

  4. Citizenship: "And are you a New Zealand citizen?"

  5. When they can move: "When are you looking to make the move?"

  6. Where: "Whereabouts in Australia are you keen to go?"

  7. Pay: "And what sort of pay are you after?"

  8. Anything to add: "Great! Is there anything else you'd like us to know?"

Questions 5, 6 and 7 give you three of your four required fields. Never skip them.

Close: qualified and can move within 3 months

"Sweet! It sounds like you'd be a really good fit for what we've got on at the moment. The next step is a proper chat with [Placer Name], one of our recruiters. They'll run you through the role in detail, the pay, where it's based and how the move over works, and they can answer any questions you've got. It takes about 15 to 20 minutes."

If they're in Auckland: "You're welcome to pop into our office for it, or we can do it on a Teams video call. Which suits you?"

If they're outside Auckland: "It's a video call on Teams."

"Are you free [today / tomorrow] at [time]?"

Lock in a time before you hang up, then:

"Perfect, I've booked you in with [Placer Name] for [day] at [time]. You'll get an email with the details shortly, and a text reminder on the morning. Have a think about any questions you've got about the move. Talk soon!"

Close: qualified but can't move within 3 months

Don't treat this as a dead end. We want them to come back to us when they're ready, not apply for a job in Australia on their own.

"No worries at all, that's really common. Just so you know, when you're ready we can have a job sorted before you even leave New Zealand, with free temporary accommodation and a free flight over. I'll touch base closer to [month]. If anything changes before then, just give me a call."

Get a month, not "sometime next year". If they can't give one, put in the earliest realistic month. See the Coaching Guide for the full Not Ready Yet script.

Close: not suitable

"Awesome, thanks so much for chatting with me today [Name]. We'll be in touch if anything comes up that matches your experience. Have a good one!"

If they don't answer

Send the No Answer SMS and set their status to Unanswered.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Calling without reading their CV and the job brief first

  • Skipping the when, where and pay questions

  • Booking a candidate who can't move within 3 months

  • Ending a call with a qualified, ready candidate without a booked time

  • Accepting "sometime next year" instead of a month

  • Forgetting to send the No Answer SMS

S7

After Every Call

After every call where you speak to the candidate, you update four fields. No exceptions, even if the candidate isn't ready, isn't suitable, or the call was short.

Tell: What You Need to Know

You don't need to write notes. Calls are recorded and transcribed automatically, because a bot connects every call when you set the candidate's status to Screening (see S6, Making the call).

These four fields are what make the system work. The readiness folders and your daily alert email are built from them. If a field is wrong or empty, the candidate lands in the wrong folder or gets missed completely.

Full Breakdown
The four required fields

You can update all four straight from the columns in your folder view.

Field What to enter
Status Change it from Screening to the tag that matches how the call went (see S1)
Notice Period The date they can move. If they give an exact date, enter it. If they only give a month, enter the 1st of that month. For example, "June 2027" becomes 1 June 2027.
Location Where in Australia they want to go, not where they live now
Ideal Salary The pay they told you they're after

About Location: older candidates may still show their current NZ address in this field. Always replace it with where they want to go in Australia.

If they didn't answer

Set the status to Unanswered and send the No Answer SMS. You can't update the other three fields because you didn't speak to them.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving the status on Screening after the call

  • Leaving a field blank because the candidate isn't ready yet

  • Entering a month without a day. Use the 1st of the month.

  • Leaving an NZ address in Location

S8

Booking the Internal Interview

When a candidate is qualified and can move within 3 months, you book them into an internal interview with the placer before you hang up.

Tell: What You Need to Know

The automation team has set it up so that any interview you book goes straight to the calendar of the placer you work with. JobAdder also emails the candidate the details automatically. You book it the same way whether the interview is on Teams or in person at our office.

From there, the placer takes over: the reminder text, the interview itself, and any rebooking if the candidate doesn't show up.

Full Breakdown

  1. Agree a day and time with the candidate while you're still on the call. Aim for today or tomorrow.

  2. Go to the job card and find the candidate

  3. Move the candidate to the Phone Interview stage

  4. Hover over the candidate's name and click the three dots beside it

  5. Select Schedule Event, then Interview

  6. Fill in the Event date, Start time and End time

  7. Under "Looks like you are scheduling a...", select Internal Meeting

  8. Click Save Event

  9. Change the candidate's status to In Process

Important: never add the client to the internal interview. This step is internal only.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Hanging up before a time is locked in

  • Booking a candidate who can't move within 3 months

  • Forgetting to move them to the Phone Interview stage

  • Adding the client to the event

  • Leaving the status on Screening instead of In Process
