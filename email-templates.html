Cool-Off Period Email

Subject
Please Take a Few Days to Consider This Opportunity
Body
Hi [Candidate Name], Good to talk today. You've done well to get to this point, not everyone does. Before we go to the client, I need you to take the next 2 days and genuinely work through what's below. I'll be calling you at the end of that period and I'll be asking you directly about each of these. So don't just skim it, actually sit down and think it through. What I need you to work through: Are you financially ready to make the move and cover yourself for the first few weeks until your first pay comes in? Are you happy to start within 4 to 6 weeks? Are you happy with the location? I've attached a cost guide — go through it line by line and put your own numbers to it. Not a rough guess. Actual numbers. Have you actually had the conversation — not just mentioned it — with your partner and family? The timeline, what it means day-to-day, all of it. This is the most common reason placements fall apart. Be straight with yourself. Does this move make sense for where you want to be in the next few years? The people who thrive over there are the ones moving toward something, not just looking for a change. Role Overview Role & Location: [insert information] Pay: [insert information] Work Type / Hours: [insert information] Scope of Work: [insert information] Why It's Worth Considering: [insert information] When I call you in 2 days, I'll be asking you directly about your financial plan, whether your family is on board, and why this role and location makes sense for you right now. Candidates who can answer these clearly are the ones who make it work over there. [Placer Name] Stand Up Recruitment

Candidate Heads-Up SMS

SMSPlacer → CandidateSend right after the endorsement email
Relevant section: Placer: P5 Client Submission
Use this when: You've just sent the endorsement email to the client and are waiting on them to confirm the exact interview time.
Convert the morning and afternoon windows to NZ time before sending. NZ is 2 to 5 hours ahead of Australia depending on the state and daylight saving — a Perth morning can be a NZ afternoon.
Message
Hi [Candidate First Name], it's [Your Name] from Stand Up Recruitment. Good news — I've sent your CV through to [Company Name] and we're lining up your interview for [Day] [Date]. We're just waiting on them to confirm the exact time, so please block out both the morning and afternoon (NZ time) that day. I'll let you know as soon as it's locked in!

CV Follow-Up — Phone Call Script

Call ScriptPlacer → ClientCall 1: 24 hours after CV · Call 2: 48 hours after Call 1
Relevant section: Placer: P5 Client Submission
Use this when: The client hasn't replied to the endorsement email. Use it for both Call 1 and Call 2. If the call goes unanswered, send Email 1 (after Call 1) or Email 2 (after Call 2) straight away.
Goal: book the interview on the call. A yes with a time, or a clean no. No maybes. If the day in your endorsement email has passed, offer a new day and re-send the Candidate Heads-Up SMS.
Script
"Hey [Client Name], it's [Placer Name] from Stand Up Recruitment." [Pleasantries] "Hey look, I sent you [Candidate First Name]'s CV — I think they'd be a perfect fit for the role you're after. Did you get a chance to have a look? Would [Day] [Date] morning or afternoon work to catch up with them?" If they are keen: Confirm the exact time before you get off the call. Pin them to a specific time, then book it in JobAdder as a Contact Candidate Meeting. If they are wishy-washy or vague: "Are you still actively hiring for this role?" If no or not a priority: "No worries — just let me know and I can send [Candidate First Name] across to another one of our clients."

Post-Client Interview Email with Candidate Details

EmailPlacer → ClientSend straight after the debrief
Relevant section: Placer: P6 Client Interview
Use this when: The client has said yes in the post-interview debrief. Send the candidate's full name and possible start date so the client can prepare the offer letter, and ask them to include the temporary accommodation and vehicle arrangements in writing.
Confirm the possible start date with the candidate before sending — allow for the 2-day offer cool off and the time they need to relocate. If the role doesn't include accommodation or a vehicle, remove that part of the email.
Subject
[Candidate Full Name] — [Role Title] Offer Details
Body
Hi [Client Name], Thanks for your time today, great to hear [Candidate First Name] is the right fit. Here are the details you'll need for the offer letter: Full name: [Candidate Full Name] Possible start date: [Start Date] One more thing: could you also add a line in the offer letter confirming the temporary accommodation and vehicle arrangements? Something like: "Temporary Accommodation: Employer will provide temporary accommodation for [X weeks] from the commencement date, at [no cost to the employee / $[amount] per week], to support relocation. Vehicle: Employer will provide a company vehicle for use in the course of employment, described as [details]." Just needs the brackets filled in with your actual details. Having it in the letter means the candidate sees it in writing upfront, and it avoids any confusion down the track. Could you let me know when we can expect the offer letter? [Placer Name]

Criminal Conviction Inquiry Reply

EmailPlacer → Candidate
Relevant section: Not tied to a section: edge case handling
Use this when: A candidate discloses a criminal conviction and asks whether they're eligible to work in Australia.
Subject
Re: Your Enquiry
Body
Hi [Name], Thanks for reaching out and for being upfront about your situation — we really appreciate that. To be honest, this is not something we are able to advise on directly as it falls outside of our expertise, but we can point you in the right direction. The good news is that having a conviction does not automatically mean you cannot enter Australia. The key steps we would suggest are: 1. Complete the online pre-travel assessment form for NZ passport holders with criminal convictions. This is run by the Australian Department of Home Affairs and will give you an idea of where you stand before you make any decisions. You can find it here: https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/help-support/departmental-forms/online-forms/travelling-with-a-criminal-conviction 2. If you are unsure about the outcome or your situation is complex, we would recommend speaking with an Australian immigration adviser who specialises in character assessments. They can give you a proper assessment of your options. Once you have clarity on your eligibility to enter Australia, we would be happy to have a conversation about opportunities that may suit your background. Hope that helps and best of luck with it! [Placer Name] Stand Up Recruitment
