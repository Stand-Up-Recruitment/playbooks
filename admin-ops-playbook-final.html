2.1.1

Timesheet Reconciliation

Every Tuesday, collect timesheets from all NZ outsourced employees, verify hours, and update the main NZ employee spreadsheet. This drives Wednesday invoicing and Thursday payrun.

Purpose and Timing

Timesheet reconciliation happens every Tuesday. All NZ outsourced employees submit their timesheets to timesheets@standuprecruitment.co.nz. You collect, verify, and record each employee hours in the main NZ employee spreadsheet. This data drives two downstream processes:

  • Wednesday: The automation uses this data to draft client invoices in Xero
  • Thursday: The payrun uses this data to calculate employee pays
Accuracy is critical. Errors in timesheet data flow through to both client invoices and employee pays. Always cross-check hours with the employee or their site manager if anything looks unusual.
Step-by-Step
1
Check the timesheets@standuprecruitment.co.nz inbox for timesheet submissions from all NZ outsourced employees. Note: some submit timesheets as attachments (PDF/image), others send a photo.
2
For each employee timesheet received:
  • Verify: name, week ending date, site/client, total hours
  • Check the hours are reasonable (standard 40 hr week, or agreed part-time hours)
  • Look for any anomalies: very short weeks, unusual overtime, public holidays
3
Open the main NZ employee spreadsheet. Find the row for each employee. Enter their hours for the week in the correct week column.
4
Save each timesheet as a PDF (if not already). Rename the file: "Employee Name — Week Ending DD MMM YYYY". Save to the timesheets folder in SharePoint.
5
If any timesheet has not arrived by Tuesday afternoon: contact the employee directly to chase it up. Do not proceed with invoicing or payroll without their hours.
6
Once all timesheets are collected and entered, do a final review of the spreadsheet: totals per client/site look correct, no employees are missing.
Dispute Resolution — Important: If any timesheet issue or dispute arises, do not reach out to Les. You are responsible for sorting it out directly with the employee and their site supervisor or site manager. Agree on the correct hours, document the outcome, and update the record accordingly.
Timesheet Issues
Missing timesheet
Chase employee by text/call on Tuesday afternoon. If still missing by Wednesday morning, use the previous week hours as an estimate and note it for correction in the following week. Do not delay invoicing.
Hours seem too high/low
Cross-check with the client or site manager. Public holidays, sick days, and RDOs can cause unusual patterns — apply the relevant leave type rather than just adjusting hours.
Employee disputes hours
Do not escalate to Les. Resolve directly with the employee and their site supervisor or site manager. Discuss the discrepancy, agree on the correct hours, and document the outcome before making any changes. Only update the timesheet once both parties have confirmed.

2.1.2

NZ Client Invoicing

Every Wednesday morning, verify and approve the auto-drafted NZ client invoices in Xero — attach timesheets, confirm hours and charge rates, and send.

Purpose & Timing

By Wednesday ~8:00 AM, the automation drafts all NZ client invoices in Xero based on the previous week's timesheets and charge rates. Your job is to verify, attach timesheets, and approve/send every invoice. This is Step 3 of the NZ weekly cycle.

Always use two screens: main NZ employee spreadsheet open on one screen, Xero on the other. You need to constantly cross-check hours and charge rates.
Step-by-Step
Approving and sending NZ invoices
1
Log into Xero. Navigate to: Sales → Invoices → Drafts. You will see all auto-drafted invoices for the week.
2
Open the first invoice. Verify:
  • Client name is correct
  • Issue date = today
  • Due date = client-specific (see table above)
  • Account = 200 Sales
  • Tax rate = 15% GST on income
  • Working hours per employee match the timesheet
  • Hourly charge rate matches the spreadsheet
  • PO number is correct (find it in spreadsheet under the client's site)
3
Click Attach Files → Upload Files. Navigate to the timesheets you saved as PDFs. Select all employees on that invoice. Click Open → ensure "Include with online invoice" is ticked.
4
Click View to preview and double-check: each employee's attached timesheet hours match the invoice line. E.g. if Jeremy worked 38.5 hrs → confirm 38.5 hrs appears on the invoice line.
5
Click Approve and Email. Repeat for all remaining draft invoices.
6
For clients with multiple sites, each site has its own PO number. Identify the site from the PO number in the invoice and attach only the relevant employees' timesheets.
Common Mistakes
Wrong due date: Every client has a different due date. Never leave it on Xero's default. Check the table above and the spreadsheet.
Forgot to tick "Include with online invoice": If not ticked, the client receives the invoice without the attached timesheet. Always tick this before saving.
Job Codes & PO Numbers: Some clients (e.g. March Cato) use job codes — each employee has a job number specific to their site. Copy the correct job code from the spreadsheet for each employee line on the invoice. If a client has multiple PO numbers, raise a separate invoice for each PO — do not combine different POs into one invoice.

Employee Entitlement Transfer (for leave payouts)

When an employee takes a leave day (sick leave, annual leave), follow these steps:

1
In the payrun, for the employee on leave: set Ordinary Time hours = 0, Work Days = 0. Keep the leave type (sick/annual) as applied. Note the net pay amount (e.g. $161.67).
2
In the Employee Entitlement account in your banking system: create a transfer for that exact net pay amount to the Ops account, referencing the employee name and reason (e.g. "Giovanni sick leave payout").
3
In Xero: reconcile this transfer as wages payable (code 814).

This process ensures the leave payment flows from the Employee Entitlement account (not the Ops account) to maintain correct accounting.

Purpose

Unscheduled pay runs are uncommon but important to know. You will be asked to run one by Les.

Staff Reimbursements

When an internal employee pays for a work-related cost from their own pocket, reimburse them through the payrun. Always get the invoice or receipt first.

When to Use

A reimbursement is needed when an internal (or outsourced) employee has paid for something work-related from their own personal bank account. Common reasons:

  • PPE purchase (safety boots, hi-vis vest, etc.)
  • Subscription or software they needed for work
  • Any authorised out-of-pocket expense
Before processing: Always get the invoice or receipt from the employee. Keep a copy on their personal file so you can refer to it later.
Step-by-Step
1
Log into Xero → Payroll → Pay Employees. Click the pay run draft you are working on.
2
Click on the employee who needs the reimbursement (e.g. Jade).
3
Scroll down. You will see a Reimbursements section. Click Add.
4
Select the correct reimbursement type:
  • Gas Allowance — for fuel/travel allowances for employees working far from home
  • Staff Reimbursement — for PPE purchases, subscriptions, or any out-of-pocket cost
5
Add a description (e.g. "PPE reimbursement — safety boots") and enter the exact amount from the invoice/receipt.
6
Click Save and Next. The reimbursement is now included in this week's payrun and will appear on the employee's payslip.
7
Let the employee know the reimbursement has been processed and will be paid with their regular wages.

To calculate time-and-a-half rate: Charge rate × 1.5

2.1.10

Manual Journal — Employee Payroll Deductions

How to correctly record training fee and loan repayment deductions from payrun in Xero using the right accounts and manual journals.

Why This Matters

When a payroll deduction for a training fee or loan repayment is processed in Xero Payroll, Xero defaults to posting it against a liability account (814 Wages Payable or 816 Wages Deductions Payable) instead of the correct expense or loan account.

If left unfixed, this overstates the training expense and the GST claimed on it, and understates the loan recovered — while leaving a meaningless balance in the liability account.

Root cause: Xero's default payroll deduction pay items post to 814 Wages Payable - Payroll or 816 Wages Deductions Payable. These are liability accounts and do not reduce the training expense or loan balance automatically.

Invoice Follow-Up Process

How Stand Up Recruitment chases overdue invoices — automated reminders at Day 3 and Day 7, followed by manual escalation from Day 10. Includes phone scripts, email templates, and the internal escalation path to Les.

Purpose

This playbook explains how Stand Up Recruitment chases overdue invoices. The first two reminders are now sent automatically by a scheduled workflow; from Day 10 onwards, the SUR accounts team is alerted and takes manual action.

The goal is to collect overdue payments quickly and consistently while maintaining strong client relationships and protecting cash flow. This playbook gives you everything you need: the timeline, the email templates the automation sends out, and the scripts and templates you use yourself once the automation hands off.

The Big Picture

The process runs across five checkpoints. The first two (Day 3 and Day 7) are fully automated — you don’t need to do anything. From Day 10 onwards, the automation sends you an internal alert and you take over.

Day 3
Automated friendly reminder email sent to client.
Day 7
Automated firm reminder email sent to client.
Day 10
Internal alert to SUR accounts team — phone call required.
Day 12
Internal alert to SUR accounts team — escalate to senior contact at client.
Day 21
Internal alert to Nihanga — brief Les to step in directly.
How the Automation Works
Schedule trigger
A scheduled workflow runs every day at 9:00 AM. It does not run on weekends.
Find overdue invoices
The workflow searches the Installments table in our Airtable CRM base for any record with Status = “Sent” and Due Date earlier than today. For each match, it looks up the corresponding invoice in Xero by Invoice ID to pull the latest payment status and details.
Day-count check
For each overdue invoice the workflow runs five day-count checks: is the invoice exactly 3, 7, 10, 12, or 21 days overdue today? Each check fires only on the exact day — not “3 or more days”. This means a single invoice triggers each reminder once.
What stops the automation
When a client pays, their invoice status in Airtable changes from “Sent” to “Paid”. Once that happens the workflow no longer picks it up — no further reminders are sent. There is no manual “stop” you need to trigger.

How It Works

Medicals are not done for every candidate — only when the client specifically requires it as a pre-employment condition. When a recruiter requests a medical, your role is to coordinate the appointment end-to-end.

Two non-negotiables: The drug test must be a lab-tested screen (not just a medical centre test), and the final report must be signed by a doctor — not a nurse alone.
Step-by-Step
1
Receive the request — Recruiter emails requesting a medical for a candidate. Note the client name and any specific assessment requirements they have specified.
2
Check candidate location — Open JobAdder, go to the candidate profile, and check where they are based (Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch, Palmerston North, or Australia).
3
Call the candidate — Get at least two available dates and two time slots. Do not lock in just one option — you need flexibility when calling the medical centre.
4
Identify the medical centre — Check the Templates tab for listed centres. If the candidate's city is not listed, search Google or Claude for "pre-employment medical centre [city]" and ring them to confirm they can meet the client's requirements.
5
Clarify payment — Before booking, confirm with the recruiter or Les: is SUR covering the full cost, 50/50, or is the candidate paying everything? This goes into the email.
6
Call the medical centre — Introduce yourself, explain you have a candidate who needs a full pre-employment medical, provide the candidate's availability, and lock in the appointment. Ask: "Will the report be signed off by a doctor?"
7
Email the medical centre — Use the email template in the Templates tab. Include all candidate details, appointment details, payment details, the full assessment requirement list, and attach the Pre-Employment Medical Assessment Form (adjust form fields to match the client's requirements if needed).
8
Email the candidate — Use the candidate email template. Include appointment date, time, location, medical centre contact details, payment arrangement, and remind them to arrive 10 minutes early and bring photo ID (driver's licence or passport).
9
Send a reminder — At least one day before the appointment, send the candidate a quick text or email reminding them of the upcoming medical. Not mandatory, but good practice.
10
Receive the medical report — The medical centre sends the completed report. Review it: look for any concerns or anything that may affect the candidate's placement. If anything notable, flag it to the recruiter or Les immediately. If the report is clean, forward it to the recruiter.
11

The Big Picture — Two Buckets of Money

Every dollar that comes into the business falls into one of two buckets, and each bucket is taxed slightly differently for GST — but exactly the same for income tax:

Bucket 1 — NZ Clients (NZD)
Standard 15% GST is added to the invoice and collected on the government's behalf.
Bucket 2 — AUS Clients (AUD)
0% GST (zero-rated) — no GST is added, because the client is outside New Zealand.

Both buckets still get combined into one company tax return each year, and the profit from both is taxed at the same 28% company tax rate. GST is the only thing that differs between the two.

Imagine two lolly jars — one labelled 'NZ' and one labelled 'Australia'. At the tollgate (GST), the NZ jar pays a toll and the Australia jar doesn't. But at the end of the year, both jars get tipped into the same big jar (the company tax return), and the taxman takes his 28% slice from the combined total.

Income Tax — The 28% Company Tax

Stand Up Recruitment Limited is taxed on its worldwide net profit at a flat company tax rate of 28%, under the Income Tax Act 2007. This applies equally to profit earned from NZ clients and Australian clients — there is no separate or higher rate for foreign-currency income. AUD amounts are simply converted to NZD and included in the same return.

How and When We Pay Income Tax
Annual Return (IR4)
Companies Income Tax Return (IR4), filed after year end (31 March balance date).
Provisional Tax
Paid in three instalments during the year as a prepayment against the year's expected tax bill: 28 August, 15 January, and 7 May (for our 31 March balance date).
Terminal Tax
The final top-up (or refund) once the actual IR4 is filed. Due 7 February the following year, or 7 April if filed through a registered tax agent.
Provisional tax is like paying for your takeaways in three chunks throughout the year instead of one huge bill at the end. Terminal tax is just squaring up the difference once we know the real total.

GST — NZ Invoices vs Australian Invoices

GST is a tax on sales, charged under the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985. We are GST-registered and file every two months on a payments basis (GST is accounted for when money is actually received/paid, not just when invoiced).

NZ Clients
15% GST added to every invoice. Collected on IRD's behalf and returned at filing.
AUS Clients
0% GST (zero-rated) under GST Act 1985, s 11A(1)(k). Every AUS zero-rated sale must still be entered on the return — it is a 0% sale, not a 'no need to report it' sale.
GST is like a toll booth on the motorway. NZ clients drive through the tollbooth and pay 15c per dollar. Australian clients get a special overseas lane pass — they drive through for free (0%) because IRD only tolls sales happening inside New Zealand. But we still have to log every car that goes through, even the free ones.
Filing GST Returns
Standard due date
28th of the month after the taxable period ends.
Exception 1
Period ending 30 November → due 15 January.
Exception 2
Period ending 31 March → due 7 May.

8.1 Why We Don't Get Taxed Twice — The Double Tax Agreement (DTA)

New Zealand and Australia have a Double Tax Agreement (in force since 2010) that decides which country gets to tax our profit. Business profits are only taxable in Australia if we have a permanent establishment there — a fixed office, or staff habitually signing contracts on our behalf on Australian soil.

Our current situation
Because Stand Up Recruitment sources and places candidates from New Zealand, with no fixed Australian office, our Australian client invoices should be taxed only once — here in NZ, at 28%.
If this ever changes (e.g. a permanent Australian office or local staff signing contracts), this needs to be revisited with our accountant immediately — it could create an Australian tax filing obligation.

Source: IRD — Double tax agreements (see Sources tab for full link)

8.2 Watch For This — Australian "No ABN Withholding"

Australian businesses are legally required to withhold 47% of any invoice over AUD $75 paid to a supplier that doesn't quote an Australian Business Number (ABN) — UNLESS that supplier is a genuine foreign business not carrying on an enterprise in Australia, which is our situation.

Current clients
Our current Australian clients are paying invoices in full with no withholding — good sign that they already understand our status.
New AUS clients
For any NEW Australian client, proactively give their accounts payable team a short written confirmation that we are a non-resident supplier not carrying on an enterprise in Australia, so their system doesn't default to withholding 47%.
Think of the ABN like a library card. Australian businesses paying another Australian business without a 'library card' (ABN) have to hand over nearly half the payment to the tax office as a safety net. But because we're a genuine overseas business, we're not supposed to be caught by that rule at all — we just sometimes need to say so in writing so their accounts team doesn't apply it by mistake.

Sources: ATO — Statement by a supplier not quoting an ABN • ATO — PAYG withholding (see Sources tab for full links)

8.3 The Zero-Rating Clause, In One Line

GST Act 1985, section 11A(1)(k): services supplied to a non-resident who is outside New Zealand at the time the service is performed are zero-rated (0% GST). Our Australian client companies are non-residents located outside NZ, so this applies directly to their invoices.

7. Foreign Currency & Exchange Rate Rules

Australian invoices are raised in AUD but settle into our NZD bank account — we don't hold a separate AUD account. This is fine and is actually simpler than running a foreign currency account.

Income Recognition
Income is recognised (for tax purposes) at the invoice date, using that day's exchange rate — not the date the cash physically lands in NZ.
Conversion Method
IRD allows daily spot rate, mid-month, end-of-month, or rolling average. Xero currently uses spot rate per invoice, which is acceptable — but whatever method is used must stay consistent.
Realised Currency Gains/Losses
Because the actual bank conversion rate on payment day is rarely identical to the invoice-date rate, a small realised currency gain or loss appears automatically. This is normal and already has its own ledger account in Xero: "Realised Currency Gains" (code 499). These gains/losses are taxable income or a tax-deductible loss respectively, under the Financial Arrangements rules.
Why it stays simple
Because we don't hold an AUD account, we avoid the more complex rolling revaluation rules that apply to foreign-currency bank balances.
It's like ordering something online from Australia and paying in AUD — the price you saw yesterday might be a few cents different from what your bank actually charges you today. That few cents' difference isn't a mistake or a tax dodge — it's just currency movement, and Xero has a special account (Realised Currency Gains) that quietly keeps track of it for us.
Worth checking separately (cost, not tax)
Banks typically apply their own margin above the wholesale exchange rate when converting incoming AUD. Comparing our bank's rate against the market mid-rate occasionally is good practice to avoid losing money to a wide margin.

Sources: IRD — Overseas currency conversion • IRD — Financial arrangements rules (see Sources tab for full links)
