1.1

Purpose, Roles & Full Process

This is the standard process for hiring internal Recruitment team staff — recruitment consultants, resourcers, and recruitment leads. It covers everything from identifying a vacancy through to a final hire decision.

What This Process Is For
  • Filtering for the right person — correct mindset, genuine attitude, can-do approach, and grit — not just a matching CV
  • Giving every interviewer or the Recruitment Team Lead a repeatable, observable scoring method
  • Keeping candidate communication professional, timely, and consistent regardless of who sends it
Response time guideline (default — confirm with Les): Aim to communicate an outcome to the candidate within 2 business days of each stage. This keeps the candidate experience professional even when the answer is no.
RoleResponsibility in This Process
Recruitment Team LeadFlags the vacancy, confirms the test-project core task for the team, sits alongside Les in the in-person interview, and has final say on the hire
Les (Founder)Attends every in-person interview, final sign-off on all hires
Nihanga (Business Ops & HR Lead)Owns this document, coordinates scheduling and logistics, sends candidate communications, runs the background and reference checks, and maintains candidate records in JobAdder
Interviewer / ScreenerRuns the phone screening and/or in-person interview per the scripts and scorecards in this document
The Full Sequence

Every internal Recruitment-team hire follows this path, start to finish:

1
Need identified — Recruitment Team Lead flags an open position
2
Job card created in JobAdder
3
Adverts posted via JobAdder to Seek, TradeMe, LinkedIn, and the company website; social content used if extra reach is needed
4
Applications land in JobAdder — CV Review to shortlist for a phone call
5
Phone Screening call — decision: book in-person interview, or decline
6
In-Person Interview (Les + Recruitment Team Lead, ~30 min) — decision: proceed, or decline
7
PRIOS Assessment and Background & Reference Checks run in parallel — decision: clear to proceed, or do not proceed
8
Scorecard Review against the Sales or Recruiter benchmark — sharpens judgment, generates follow-up questions
9
Test Project (3 hours, recruitment-specific tasks) — the heaviest-weighted step
10
Career ladder & commission structure shared with the successful candidate
11
Final decision → Hire / Decline / Team discussion
Training note: This is a filter at every stage, not a funnel you have to complete. If someone is clearly wrong at any point, end it there — don't carry them forward out of politeness.

3.1

CV Review

Screen every application against the role brief before any contact is made with the candidate.

Purpose

The first filter in the funnel. Applications come in through JobAdder from every posting channel (Seek, TradeMe, LinkedIn, company website). Screen each CV against the role brief before booking a phone call — this keeps the phone screening stage focused on genuinely relevant candidates.

What to Check
1
Relevant experience or transferable skills against the role brief
2
Employment history — gaps, tenure patterns, career trajectory
3
Whether their address/suburb is already stated (saves asking again at phone screening)
4
Whether the CV already lists two references (name, contact, company, position) — note this either way, it matters at the in-person interview stage
5
Basic logistics that would rule someone out immediately (e.g. wrong location, wrong work rights)
Note: If references aren't listed on the CV, don't chase them now — that request happens at the in-person interview stage (3.3). Just flag it as missing so the interviewer knows to ask.

3.2

Phone Screening

A quick two-way screen: motivation, basic fit, and logistics. 5-10 minutes — this call is deciding whether to bring the candidate in for an in-person interview, not deciding a hire.

Opener
“Hi, is this [Name]? You applied here at Stand Up Recruitment for the [role] role — have you got a quick five minutes to talk?”
Training note: They applied, so this isn't a cold call — don't over-explain who you are or what Stand Up does yet. Confirm identity, confirm the role, ask for the five minutes, then move straight into Q1.
#QuestionWhy It's Asked
1Why are you looking for a new role right now?Listen for a genuine, specific reason vs. something vague or evasive.
2Are you looking for a full-time role?Non-negotiable filter for this role — part-time/casual-only ends the process here.
3Where do you live? (only ask if not already on their CV)Skip if already on the CV. A reliability filter on commute distance.
4Are you a NZ citizen? (only ask if not already clear) — if not, what visa category?Don't ask a NZ citizen about visa status. If on a visa, the category affects start timing and role eligibility.
5What are you looking to earn — and what's the minimum that would make the move worthwhile?Ask plainly so you're not wasting time later. If outside budget, better to know now.
6What's your notice period — and realistically, how soon could you start?Tells you how live they actually are. A mismatched notice period is a genuine constraint, not just detail.
Scoring the Call Itself
How they show up on the call matters as much as what they say.
SignalPassFail
Energy / tonePresent, engaged, sounds like they want to be thereFlat, monotone, sounds checked out
ListeningAnswers the actual question askedRambles, pitches themselves, ignores the question
Conversation flowCan hold a back-and-forth naturallyYou're doing all the work to keep it moving
DirectnessGives real, specific answersHedges, vague, non-answers
Training note: Communication is the job. For a recruiter, this table isn't just a screening filter — it's the closest thing to a live audition. If someone nails all four signals, don't just say you'll be in touch — lock in an interview time slot before you hang up.
Close
“Look, based on our chat I'd love to get you in for a quick in-person interview — have you got some time this week? Would [Day, Time] or [Day, Time] work for you?”

If you need more time to decide:

“Appreciate it — I'll be in touch within [X days] either way.”
Lock it in: If you're locking in a time, get the day and time confirmed before you hang up, then send the Interview Confirmation email as a calendar invite so it lands in their diary, not just their inbox.
Decision
  • Sounds good, strong energy & communication → lock in an interview time on the call, then send the Interview Confirmation email as a calendar invite
  • Sounds clearly bad → decline, note reason

3.3

In-Person Interview

~30 minutes, Les + Recruitment Team Lead. Run it like a conversation, not an interrogation — let the candidate talk more than you do.

How this should feel: Let silences sit instead of rushing to fill them, and when something real comes up — a name, a hobby, a frustration, a throwaway comment — stay on it a beat longer before moving to the next line. That's usually where the more honest, less rehearsed answers come from.

Decision

  • Fail → let them go warmly on the day, note the reason on file, and send the "Rejected — After Interview" email within 1-2 business days
1. Icebreaker & Getting to Know Them
“Before we get into anything — just tell me about yourself. Not your CV, just you. What are you about?”
“What does life outside of work look like at the moment — who's in your corner, family, flatmates, whoever keeps you grounded?”
“What attracted you to apply at Stand Up Recruitment?”
Follow their energy: If they light up talking about a partner, kids, a parent, a sport, or a mate, stay there an extra beat and ask one genuine follow-up before moving on. This is about understanding who they are, not assessing their personal circumstances — a short answer is a fine answer, don't push.
2. Hunger & Competitiveness
“What's something you've worked really hard to get good at — and what drove you to keep going?”
“Tell me about a time you had to put in serious extra hours or effort to hit something that mattered — what did that actually look like, and how did you feel about it afterward?”
3. Overcoming Challenges
“What's the biggest challenge you've had to work through — whether in your studies, work, or life in general? How did you approach it?”
4. Handling Feedback
“Tell me about a time someone gave you tough feedback, or pulled you up on something you got wrong. What did they say, and what did you do with it?”
Training note: You're testing for a can-do reaction to being corrected, not perfection in the story. Defensiveness or blame-shifting here is a real flag — this role will involve regular feedback.
5. Team & Resilience Background
“Have you ever been part of a team — sport, work, community, anything — where you had to push through something hard together? Tell me about it and what it taught you about yourself.”
6. Role-Relevant Scenario
“Quick scenario — [Recruitment Team Lead to run a live scenario relevant to the role — see Appendix].”
Training note: Zero role-specific experience required — this is testing raw thinking and composure under a bit of pressure, not industry knowledge.
7. About Us & The Role
Sell after testing, not before: Selling first risks candidates shaping earlier answers to match what they've just heard you're excited about.
8. Closing
“Is there anything about you that we wouldn't know from your CV — something you want us to know before we wrap up?”
“What questions do you have for us?”
“Just a practical one before we wrap up — what's your notice period at the moment, and realistically, how soon could you start if this moves forward?”
9. Next Steps
“Before you go, here's what happens next on our end: if we'd like to move forward, we run a criminal record check, two reference checks, and two independent checks — that's standard for every hire. If you haven't already sent through two referees, could you flag your two most recent supervisors for us?”
New step — request what's missing, right here: If they haven't provided referees yet, get two now — ideally direct supervisors from their two most recent roles. If you don't already have what you need for the criminal record check (full name, date of birth, residential address, and driving licence number), ask for it here rather than chasing it later by email.
Locking In the Test Project

If the interview has gone really well and you're confident in them, don't wait — ask for their availability for the test project and lock in a date and time before they leave. If it's a clear no, let them go warmly; we'll follow up with a decision by email within 1-2 business days either way.

Training note: Locking in the project date on the spot for a strong candidate keeps momentum and avoids a scheduling back-and-forth over email. If it's a no, don't leave it open-ended — a clean, kind close beats a vague "we'll be in touch."

3.4

PRIOS Assessment & Background/Reference Checks

Runs after the in-person interview passes and before the test project. Allow 2-5 business days depending on referee availability. Owned by Nihanga.

Why These Run Together

The PRIOS Assessment and the background/reference checks both start once the in-person interview passes, and both run in parallel — neither waits on the other. This saves real time in the funnel compared to running everything sequentially.

What's Covered
CheckWhat It IsWho Runs It
PRIOS AssessmentCandidate completes the Principles Assessment ($100/candidate) onlineCandidate (self-directed)
Criminal Record CheckMinistry of Justice check, once candidate gives written consentNihanga
Reference Checks (x2)Calls to the two referees the candidate providedNihanga
Independent Checks (x2)Informal checks with people who worked alongside the candidate but weren't nominated as refereesNihanga
No exceptions: Every candidate who passes the in-person interview goes through all five checks below — no shortcuts for a candidate who "seems great."
Why It Sits Here

The assessment costs $100 per candidate. Running it only after CV, phone, and in-person screening means we only pay for people who've already proven themselves worth the deeper look — cost-efficient, at the trade-off of not protecting Les's own interview time as early as it could.

What It Measures

17 traits across three orientations: Cognitive, Interpersonal, and Motivational. It's a read on style and thinking tendencies, not skill or track record — the result feeds directly into Scorecard Review (3.5).

Checks 2 & 3 — Reference Checks (x2)

A minimum of two reference checks — both from supervisors the candidate provided during the in-person interview.

The Reference Check Rhythm

Do not call one referee and wait to hear back — batch your calls and work through the list efficiently.

TimeAction
MorningCall all referees on your list
AfternoonRecall anyone who did not answer in the morning
After 2nd no answerSend the reference no-answer SMS and wait for a reply
The Reference Check Call Script

Keep it casual. You want the referee to open up naturally — not feel like they're being formally interviewed.

StepApproachScript
1. OpenOwn the familiarity. Say their name first — confident, not a question.“[Referee First Name].” (They respond) “Hey, it's [Your Name] — [Candidate Full Name] gave me your number, said it's okay to run a bit of a reference check.”
2. The rundownLet them talk. Ask it open, then stop talking. Dig deeper on anything interesting.“Can you give me a full rundown please — the good, the bad, and the ugly?”
3. Skills & experienceGet specific to the role. Don't accept a general answer. Push once if too smooth.“What kind of work was he actually doing for you?” “Was he good at it?” “Any muck-ups or mistakes worth mentioning?”
4. If hesitantDon't push if they seem cagey or want to loop in HR. Respect it and note it as a flag.“No worries at all — appreciate you letting me know. If you think of anything after we hang up, feel free to flag it.”
5. The closeSay it slow. Let the cost of a bad hire land before the ask. Don't fill the silence after.“Look [Name], I'm sure you've made bad hires before and know how much it can cost. With that in mind, do you think [Candidate Full Name] would be a good addition to my company?”
While They're Talking, Listen For
  • Attitude — on the tools, with the team, under pressure
  • Attendance — reliable, punctual, any red flags
  • Anything volunteered unprompted — usually the most honest signal

If they're short or vague, try: “Yeah? What was that like day to day?” or “Anything stand out, good or bad?”

Handling Red Flags & Lukewarm Responses
OutcomeWhat to Do
1 red flag or lukewarm responseAsk the candidate for an additional referee — don't tell them why
2 or more red flags out of 3Do not progress. Inform the candidate we will not be moving forward
All clearProceed to the independent checks, the police (criminal record) check, and CV formatting
Checks 4 & 5 — Independent Checks (x2)

Two further, informal checks with people who worked alongside the candidate but weren't nominated as referees. Referees are usually a candidate's strongest supporters — friendly, positive, naturally biased. An independent check gets an honest, unprimed reaction instead: the person you reach isn't expecting your call and hasn't been coached on what to say.

How to Find Them
1
Go through the candidate's CV and identify their two most recent employers, excluding whichever companies their own referees came from
2
Search for the company's phone number online and call the general line — don't use a number the candidate has given you
3
Ask reception (or whoever answers) to put you through to someone who can speak to the candidate's time there — ideally a supervisor, team lead, or manager (a colleague is fine too)
4
If nobody currently there remembers them, ask if anyone still on staff was working during that period
5
If you can't get anyone useful at that company, move to the next most recent employer on the CV and repeat, until you land two independent checks
Training note: Use the same call script as the nominated reference checks — the goal is the same honest rundown, just from someone the candidate didn't hand-pick. Be professional and discreet; you're checking work history, not gossiping.
Check 1 — Criminal Record Check

Requested through the Ministry of Justice Criminal Record Check service once the candidate has given written consent. Results are typically returned within a few working days.

Training note: A conviction on record isn't an automatic decline — read it in context (what, how long ago, how relevant to the role) and discuss with Les before deciding either way. What we're screening for is undisclosed risk, not making moral judgements on someone's past.
Stage Checklist
CheckResult
Criminal record check requested — cleared, or flagged item discussed with LesClear / Discuss
Reference 1 completed — no concerns raisedClear / Discuss
Reference 2 completed — no concerns raisedClear / Discuss
Independent check 1 completed — no concerns raisedClear / Discuss
Independent check 2 completed — no concerns raisedClear / Discuss
Rule: All five Clear = proceed to Scorecard Review and the Test Project. Any Discuss = talk it through with Les before booking the test project — don't default to decline, and don't default to progress either.
Decision
  • All clear → send "Selected for Project" email, book the Test Project
  • Concern raised → discuss with Les before proceeding; if not proceeding, send "Rejected — After Checks" email

Score the candidate against the relevant benchmark (Sales or Recruiter) using the scorecard workbook. Used to sharpen judgment and generate interview follow-ups — not a pass/fail gate.

Highest-Weighted Traits

Nurturing (14%), Detailed & Reliable (11%), and Extraverted (11%) carry the most weight — these are the traits most worth probing hardest for in interview follow-ups.

Scoring a Candidate
1
Fill in candidate name, position, interviewer, and date at the top of the relevant tab (Sales or Recruiter)
2
For each trait, enter a score from 0-100 in the yellow cell — use the candidate's real PRIOS percentile if they completed the assessment, or an honest interview estimate guided by the Interview Signal column if they haven't
3

4
The Fit Index at the bottom is the candidate's weighted score divided by the benchmark's weighted score — 100% means they match the target profile; over 100% means they exceed it on the traits weighted most heavily
Use it right: Use a low Fit Index or a large Gap on a specific trait as a prompt for a sharper interview question — not as an automatic disqualifier.

3.6

Test Project

The heaviest-weighted step in the funnel. Real, on-the-job style work sample — approx. 3 hours, in the recruiters' room.

Why It's Weighted Heaviest

The test project is the best available predictor of on-the-job performance — carrying roughly 40-50% of the final call (see 4.1 for the full weighting breakdown). Every item is scored Yes/No — observable, not a feeling. Two different people running this should land on the same score for the same candidate.

Confirmed Recruitment core task: The task below is already confirmed for the Recruitment team (see Appendix A for the matching interview scenario). If it ever changes, confirm the updated version with Nihanga before first use.
The Three Parts
PartWhat It TestsRule
1. Phone Calls (hard gate)Live calls to real pipeline leads2+ "No"s → end the test project here
2. Systems TaskCore system navigation (e.g. JobAdder)All three checked = Pass; any unchecked = Discuss
3. Ambient Team Fit"Would you want to work next to this person every day?"Majority Yes = Pass
Hard gate: 2 or more "No"s on the signals below → end the test project here. Do not continue to Part 2.
Setup

Real candidates from the pipeline — laborer-tier leads only, not high-quality leads. Give them the script but minimal extra info. There's no right or wrong answer — this is purely testing how they think on their feet, adjust, and persuade.

What You're Watching For
1
Stayed on the call and kept trying after the first "not interested" (didn't go silent or give up)
2
Adjusted their approach when the first attempt didn't land
3
Sounded natural, not robotically reading the script
4
Asked a real follow-up question instead of just pitching
Part 2 — Systems Task

Pure systems/navigation test — can they figure out a core system task unaided (e.g. JobAdder, Xero, Airtable, Notion — whichever the role uses day-to-day).

1
Completed all assigned tasks without being shown twice
2
Asked sensible clarifying questions when stuck (rather than guessing blindly or freezing)
3
Finished within a reasonable time (Recruitment Team Lead to set benchmark)
Part 3 — Ambient Team Fit

No separate task needed — because they're in the recruiters' room for the full session, the team is naturally exposed to them. At the end, ask every recruiter who shared the room with them, independently, the same question:

“Would you want to work next to this person every day?”

Majority Yes = Pass.

4.1

Stage Weighting & Final Decision

The scorecard is one input among several — not a stand-alone pass/fail filter. Here's roughly how much each stage should influence the final call.

StageWeightWhy
Assessment / Scorecard~15-20%Signal on style and thinking patterns; flags things to probe further.
In-Person Interview~30-40%Read on how they actually think and respond, live.
Test Project~40-50%Best available predictor of on-the-job performance.
Background & Reference Checks

These run in parallel with the assessment step and act as a corroborating check, rather than carrying their own separate weight in this table — but a Discuss-level concern still stops the process until Les weighs in (see 3.4).

OutcomeResult
Test Project's Part 1 gate failsNo hire — stop immediately; send the "Rejected — After Project" email the next business day
Part 1 gate passes, Parts 2 and 3 both passHire — share career ladder & commission structure (4.3), then send offer
Part 1 gate passes, one of Parts 2/3 failsDiscuss as a team — not an automatic yes or no
Ambient Team Fit fails on its own, everything else passesDiscuss as a team before deciding — this is a real signal, not a tie-breaker to ignore
Bottom line: No single stage — including the scorecard — is a stand-alone hire/no-hire gate on its own (the Test Project's Part 1 phone-call gate is the one true hard gate in the whole funnel). Everything else feeds into one overall judgement call.

5.2

A Word of Caution

What the PRIOS Assessment measures — and what it should never be used as a substitute for.

Important: The PRIOS Assessment measures style and thinking tendencies, not skill or track record. It should always be used alongside real results, references, and the test project — never as a stand-alone hire/no-hire decision on its own.
In Practice

A strong Fit Index is a reason to feel more confident, not a reason to skip the Test Project or background/reference checks. A weak Fit Index is a reason to probe harder in interview, not an automatic decline. The Test Project's live, observable performance is always the heaviest input — see 4.1 for the full weighting.
