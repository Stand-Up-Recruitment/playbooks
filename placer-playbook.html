Your day

Your calendar runs your day. The sourcer fills it, you work it.

  1. Before 8:00
    Team meeting, then check your calendar

    Send the Meeting Day Reminder text to everyone booked with you today.

  2. Morning
    Reference calls

    Trigger the reference calls for everyone who needs them. They run while you get on with your day.

  3. All day
    Interviews, clients, offers

    Run your booked internal interviews. Call clients who haven't replied to a CV. Set up any new job that lands. Handle offers the same day they arrive.

  4. Afternoon
    Catch up and chase

    Retrigger any reference calls that weren't answered. Do your Day 2 cool off calls. Follow up anything waiting on a client or a signature.

A new job lands

Sales closes a client and the job is emailed to recruiters. Take it, understand it, then hand it to your sourcer.

  1. Assign it to your workflow in JobAdder. Jobs come through without one.
  2. Watch the sales call video in Airtable, then download the transcript.
  3. Make the interview guide. Give the job description and transcript to the Interview Guide Project in Claude. Keep the guide for every interview on this job.
  4. Check the pay lines up across the job description, the sales call and the guide. Anything off goes to the sales team before you go further.
  5. Post the job ad using the Job Ads Project. Name it Role | State.
  6. Create a task for your sourcer so they start calling tomorrow.

The internal interview

The most important conversation before the client meets them. On Teams, or at the office if they're in Auckland. Always recorded, so no notes.

Before you start

  • You've read the sourcer's call transcript in the Activity tab
  • You've got the interview guide for this job open
  • You've checked for client feedback on earlier candidates
  • You've started recording in Teams before the first question

How it runs

  1. The real why. Ask why they want to move, then dig 2 to 3 more times until you get the real reason.
  2. Sell the role. The company, the work, the pay, the lifestyle.
  3. Check they're keen. If they're hesitant, find out why.
  4. Screening questions. Timeframe, family, anything serious, medical, ACC claims, passport, holidays. If they're staying with family, confirm it.
  5. The money talk. Moving costs around $5,000 before the first pay. Does that line up?
  6. Their experience, then the 3 technical questions from your guide.
  7. The close. Explain the 2-day cool off. Get 2 referees and the 6 police check details before you hang up.
Watch: an internal interview
Internal interview script

"Hey [Name], good to meet you properly. Thanks for making the time. This is just a chat so I can give you a proper rundown of the role and see if it's a good fit for where you're at."

The real why

"What's making you want to make the move to Australia?"

Then: "What would that extra money actually let you do?" / "Is there something specific driving that?" / "Why does that matter to you right now?"

Screening questions

  • "If you were to move, what's your timeframe?"
  • "Would you be moving over on your own, or bringing family with you?"
  • "We don't need to know your criminal history, but is there anything serious that could stop you from moving to Australia for work?"
  • "Any medical conditions or restrictions we should be aware of that might affect site work?"
  • "Have you ever had an ACC claim for an injury? Anything serious that might affect your work?"
  • "Do you have a valid passport with at least 6 months validity?"
  • "Do you have any planned holidays or commitments in the next few months?"

Accommodation check (if the sourcer's notes say they're staying with family)

"I saw in our notes that you have family in [city] and you're staying with them. Is that correct?"

Yes: move on. Changed their mind? Ask: "How are you going to sort your accommodation?" Happy to find their own: move on. Otherwise, tell them if the job comes with accommodation, or that they'll need to find their own because it doesn't.

The money talk

"Moving over costs more than most people expect. Your flight alone is $500 to $1,000. Accommodation runs $350 to $850 a week. Most people need a car within the first few weeks. So realistically, before your first pay comes in, you're looking at around $5,000 all up. Does that line up with where you're at financially?"

The close

"Before we move you to the client interview stage, I want you to take the next 2 days to properly think this through. Relocating to Australia is a big decision. I'll send you an email with a summary of the role and the relocation information. We'll touch base in 2 days and decide together whether to move forward."

References and police check

Start both the same day as the interview, during the cool off. Both must be clear before the client sees them.

  • 2 references from the candidate, connected by the bot
  • 1 independent reference you find and call yourself
  • Police check submitted in Airtable with all 6 details
Watch: reference checks

The police check form has payment details, so it's never recorded. Sit with an experienced placer the first time.

Exact clicks: triggering the reference calls
  1. On the job card, hover over the candidate's name and click the three dots.
  2. Reference Check, then Reference Check Order Form.
  3. Referee's name, referee's number, your number. Save.
  4. Do it again for the second referee. Save.
  5. Move the candidate to the Reference Check stage. That starts the calls.
  6. Your phone rings. Answer and press any key. The bot rings referee 1, then referee 2.

No answer? Retrigger in the afternoon. Still nothing after the 2nd try, send the Reference No Answer SMS.

Reference check script

"[Referee First Name]." ... "Hey, it's [Your Name]. [Candidate Full Name] gave me your number, said it's okay to run a bit of a reference check."

"Can you give me a full rundown please? The good, the bad, and the ugly."

Then their skills, how good they were, and any muck-ups. Too smooth? "Genuinely no issues at all?"

"Hey [Referee Name], I'm pretty much looking for an excuse not to hire [Candidate First Name], because you know a bad hire costs a lot of money and time, right? Can you give me any reasons not to hire [him/her]?"

"Would you rehire [him/her]?"

Then stay quiet. For the independent referee, open with: "Hey, it's [Your Name] from Stand Up Recruitment. I'm running a reference check on [Candidate Full Name], who I understand worked with you at [Company]. Have you got a few minutes?"

Get it signed

From a cleared candidate to a placement, in this order.

  1. Format the CV in the CV Formatting Project. First name only, no contact details.
  2. Send it to the client with the endorsement email, ending "Will [Day] [Date] morning or afternoon work for you?" Text the candidate a heads-up.
  3. No reply? Call. Call at 24 hours, then again 48 hours later. Book the interview on the call.
  4. Book the client interview as a Contact Candidate Meeting, so they get separate invites. Sit in and debrief.
  5. The offer lands. Run the Offer Letter Review. Reply to the client the same day.
  6. Call the candidate, then send the offer through DocuSeal. They get 48 hours to read it and talk it over.
  7. Once they sign, book the Welcome Call with the pastoral care team. Ask for referrals before you hop off.
  8. Place them in JobAdder. That starts onboarding. You're done.

Pick the stage

They didn't show up
  1. Send the No-Show Interview Email and SMS.
  2. Set their status to No Show-Up.
  3. When they reply, rebook them and set them back to In Process.
Strong candidate, keen and ready
  1. Send the cool off and document request emails.
  2. Start references and the police check today.
  3. Book the Day 2 call.
Good, but their move date has slipped
  • Update their Notice Period. The folders move them automatically.
Not right for this job
  • Set Unsuccessful - Internal on the job card. Never click Reject.
  • Decide whether they could suit another job.
Hesitant on the Day 2 call
  • Give them 2 more days. Don't push for a decision.
A referee didn't answer
  • Retrigger the call in the afternoon.
  • Still nothing after the 2nd try, send the Reference No Answer SMS.
One red flag or a lukewarm answer
  • Ask the candidate for one more referee. Don't tell them why.
Two or more red flags, or a serious police result
  • Don't progress them. Set Unsuccessful - Internal on the job card.
  • Let the candidate know we won't be moving forward.
All clear
  • Format the CV and send it to the client.
No reply to the CV
  1. 24 hours: call. No answer, send Email 1: Just Tried Calling.
  2. 48 hours later: call again. No answer, send Email 2: Still Need a [Role Title]?
  3. Still nothing: put the candidate forward to another client.
The client said yes
  1. Send the Post-Client Interview Email with Candidate Details.
  2. Ask when the offer letter is coming.
  3. Call the candidate with the good news.
The client said no
  1. Ask what was missing, and log it on the job for the next candidates.
  2. Set Unsuccessful - Client on the job card.
  3. Send a rejection email with their actual feedback.
The client went quiet after the interview
  • Follow the contract follow-up loop. See Look it up.
The review flagged a hard stop
  • Stop. Raise it with the client before the candidate sees anything. A salary with no paid overtime is always a hard stop.
The review flagged things to discuss
  • Raise the [Recommended] ones with the client. The [Optional] ones are your call.
48 hours passed and they haven't signed
  • Give them a call and find out where they're at.
They signed
  1. Book the Welcome Call and send the Welcome Call Email.
  2. Once you have the signed contract and ID, place them in JobAdder.

Placing a candidate: the five fields

Candidate's profile, three dots, Place Candidate.

FieldWhat to put in
Start dateThe date the candidate and client agreed
Base salaryThe hourly rate, for example 47.50
Total feeSee the fee below
Application statusThe relocation package (East Coast, SA, WA or none), or Replacement
Signed offerThe contract signed by the candidate
Working out the fee
Fee typeFormula
Flat fee$16,000 AUD
15%, hourlyHourly rate × weekly hours × 52 × (1 + super rate) × 15%
15%, salary, super on topSalary × (1 + super rate) × 15%
15%, salary, super includedSalary × 15%

Example: $47.50 an hour, 40 hours, 12% super. $47.50 × 40 × 52 = $98,800. × 1.12 = $110,656. × 15% = $16,598.40.

Super rates differ between clients. If it's not in the job description, ask the client.
