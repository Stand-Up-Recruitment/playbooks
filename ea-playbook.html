What This Role Really Is

Most people misunderstand what an Executive Assistant does. They assume an EA is someone who "helps," "supports," or "organises." But at Stand Up Recruitment, the EA role is built off a very different foundation.

Here, the EA is not a helper. The EA is the operating system behind the CEO.

Your purpose is simple: Buy back the founder's time — every single day — and compound that time into more execution, more clarity, more growth, and less chaos.

A world-class EA:

  • Thinks ahead, not behind
  • Protects time like revenue depends on it — because it does
  • Filters information so only what matters reaches the CEO
  • Turns messy workflows into clean systems
  • Eliminates repetitive decisions
  • Brings structure to days that would otherwise slip into chaos
  • Spots risk early and keeps everyone accountable
  • Makes sure the CEO is never blindsided

Test Scenario 1

It's your first day. You've received access to Slack but Teams is not working. Les has a client call in 2 hours and you need to send a meeting invite. What do you do?

Model Answer: Contact the IT/Teams access person immediately (or Les directly if IT is unavailable). Document the issue in Trello. If Teams cannot be resolved in time, inform Les immediately so he can decide to reschedule or use an alternative meeting method. Do not attempt to send the invite without confirmed access — a broken Teams link is worse than a delayed one.
Test Scenario 2

Les asks you to "just quickly sort out the inbox." You log in and find 47 unread emails. How do you approach it?

Model Answer: Start with the oldest email and work toward the newest. Categorise each into Review (Les's input needed), Actioned (handled), or Waiting On (awaiting reply). Reply to anything within your authority. Flag receipts. Unsubscribe from irrelevant lists. Aim for inbox zero. Never re-read emails more than once — decide and move.

The Five Foundational Mindsets
1. You Are the Firewall
Everything that reaches Les has already passed through you. Filter noise, digest information, remove clutter, and present only what matters.
2. Guardian of Time
Time is the CEO's most valuable asset. Every item on the calendar must be intentional, not accidental.
3. Operate 2–4 Weeks Ahead
Where most people react to the day, you operate with foresight. A world-class EA is never surprised.
4. Systemise What You Touch
"If you touch it twice, you systemise it." Templates, SOPs, automations, checklists, delegation pathways.
5. Bring Certainty
Your communication removes ambiguity. Your updates reduce cognitive load. You bring structure to uncertainty.
The 10-80-10 Rule

Les handles the first 10% (vision) and the last 10% (final review). You handle the middle 80% (execution). This is your zone of responsibility.

Dan Martell's 12 EA Hacks
  1. Grant full inbox access — EA reads, filters, and replies. Les only sees what needs him.
  2. Document via video — Every process recorded on Loom. No verbal-only SOPs.
  3. Weekly sync — 30–60 min dedicated time to align, review, and improve systems.
  4. Continuously improve — Every system gets better each week. Nothing stays static.
  5. Design the Perfect Week — Calendar blocked by category before the week begins.
  6. Context notes for every meeting — Who, why, what Les needs to know, anticipated questions.
  7. Trip files for travel — Master document pack built before every trip. Offline. Complete.
  8. Preloaded Year — Key dates, campaigns, travel, and reviews planned 12 months ahead.
  9. Reroute communication — EA's contact details go on outbound comms where possible.
  10. Close the loop daily — End of day update every day. Les never wonders what happened.
  11. EA speaks in decisions, not updates — Don't tell Les what happened. Tell him what you need from him.
  12. The Buyback Mindset — Every hour bought back from Les = more revenue.

EA Playbook 03

Inbox Control

The inbox is not where chaos lives — it is where you create clarity. Les only sees what requires his judgement. Everything else is processed by you.

The Two Daily Sweeps
Morning Sweep
8:00 AM — resets the board. Read every email, decide instantly, categorise into the appropriate folder, reply on Les's behalf, flag receipts, log updates to CEO Dashboard.
Afternoon Sweep
2:00 PM — prevents overnight build-up. Same process. Never re-read emails more than once.
Inbox Folder System
FolderPurpose
ReviewEmails requiring Les's input, decision, or review
ActionedEmails fully handled by EA — audit trail
Waiting OnEA is waiting on a reply or follow-up before proceeding
Step-by-Step Processing Workflow
  1. Start with the oldest email, work toward the newest
  2. Categorise and sort each email using the folder system above
  3. Flag anything requiring Les's input → move to Review folder
  4. Unsubscribe from irrelevant mailing lists (unless from close partners)
  5. Tag receipts and move to Receipts folder
  6. Reply to anything within your authority using approved templates
  7. Label and archive emails once handled — nothing stays in the main inbox
  8. Check Spam folder once per sweep
  9. Build a tone doc — log how Les wants things said. Never ask the same question twice
  10. Inbox should be close to zero by end of day

Emails — Pass Through Rules
NEVER pass through to Les: Admin requests · Scheduling · Follow-ups · Document asks · Low-value questions · Repetitive information · Anything you can resolve yourself
ALWAYS pass through to Les (with a summary): Strategic decisions · Sensitive communications · High-value client relationships · Complex business judgment · Anything tied to sales or key partnerships
Writing on Les's Behalf

Tone must be: direct, clear, no fluff, professional but warm, calm and confident.

Study Les's writing style. Keep a tone document — log phrases he uses, his email openings, how he handles difficult messages. Never deviate from his voice.

EA Playbook 04

Calendar Command

The calendar is not a schedule — it is the engine room that drives the entire organisation. EA is 100% in control of Les's calendar.

How to Design the Week

Begin the week by scanning the coming 7 days. Ask:

  • Where are the choke points and long days?
  • Where does Les need prep or buffer time?
  • Where are meetings too close together?
  • What sessions can be batched?
  • Which meetings require pre-reading or an agenda?
  • What should be pushed to the following week?

Then block: strategic time, deep work, recruiting blocks, client-facing sessions, internal communication, catch-up time, review time, and recovery time.

Meeting Setup Requirements

Every meeting must have: agenda, attendees, purpose, anticipated questions Les might be asked, and a context note added to his calendar event.

Double Invite Rule:
1. One invite with Les + attendees (what they see)
2. One invite for Les only, with full internal details (hidden from attendees)

Notify Les ASAP if any meeting is cancelled. Rebook the slot where possible.
SOP: Booking a Teams Meeting (Sales Call)
  1. Receive booking request — Check email or Teams message from Les
  2. Identify client details — name, company, email, and context
  3. Check Les's calendar — find suitable slots. Avoid back-to-back meetings
  4. Open Microsoft Teams — navigate to Calendar view
  5. Create New Meeting — click "New Meeting"
  6. Meeting title format: "Sales Call — [Client Name] — [Company Name]"
  7. Add attendees — Les's email and the client's email
  8. Select date & time — confirm time zone alignment
  9. Ensure Teams link is enabled — auto-generates
  10. Write meeting description — include purpose, prep materials, and Teams link
  11. Set reminder — 15-minute reminder for Les
  12. Send invitation
  13. Confirm in calendar — verify meeting appears correctly
  14. Notify Les — send quick confirmation with meeting details
  15. Create context note — add client background to Les's calendar event
  16. Schedule follow-up reminders — 24-hour and 3–4 hour reminder emails
Key Rules: Always check time zones for international clients · Default meeting length: 30 minutes · Leave 15-minute buffer gaps between meetings · Double-check email addresses before sending

EA Playbook 05

Communication Flow

A CEO must never chase updates. You eliminate that by creating certainty through communication — not volume, but clarity.

SOP: Morning Rundown with Les

Duration: 20–30 min · Daily · Before team standup

EA leads this meeting. Les reacts. If it's not in Trello — capture it live, during the rundown.

Step 1 — Open Trello (2 min)
Board must be open and scanned before Les sits down. Do not be sorting cards while he waits.
Step 2 — Review Yesterday (5 min)
Walk the Done list. Ask: "Anything from yesterday I need to pick up?" "Any decisions land after hours?" "Anything still open that's blocking you?"
Step 3 — Build Today's Plan (10 min)
Present top items. Move into Doing together. Ask: "Here are your top 3 today — does this match what's in your head?" Capture new items into Trello Inbox immediately.
Step 4 — Flags & Blockers (5 min)
Decisions Les needs to make today · Things waiting on others · Anything risky or unclear.
Step 5 — EA Task Review (5 min)
Present your Doing list only — one line each: what it is, who it's waiting on, whether you need anything from Les to unblock it. Ask: "Is there anything on my list you want me to prioritise differently?"
Step 6 — Close (2 min)
Confirm Les's top 3. Confirm your top 3. Execute.
SOP: Daily Team Standup

Duration: 20–30 min · Daily · Run by EA · Les present (transitioning out)

Per department, 3–5 min each

Q1
"What did you complete yesterday?" — facts only, no storytelling
Q2
"What are your top 3 priorities today?" — 3 max, not 7
Q3
"What's blocking you or slowing you down?" — if none, move on immediately
Q4
"Do you need Les's input on anything today?" — yes or no, queue it
Les's Slot — 5 min max: "Les — we have [X] items queued for you." Les addresses queued items only. EA notes decisions in Trello immediately. When done — EA closes the meeting. Not Les.
Transition Plan: Weeks 1–2: Les present for full meeting · Weeks 3–4: Les joins for his slot only · Month 2+: Les receives written summary from EA only
Kill Tangents Fast

The moment a conversation goes deeper than 30 seconds between two people, cut it:

"Let's take that offline — note it in Trello and follow up after."

Do this confidently, not apologetically. You are running the meeting.

EA Playbook 06

CRM & Sales Support

Every client call gets logged accurately in Airtable. The right status is set after every interaction. Nothing falls through the cracks.

The Golden Rules
  • Les sends one Slack message after every call. EA actions everything from 7am.
  • EA updates Airtable within 30 minutes of starting the day
  • EA cannot set Closed or Key Client without Les's instruction
  • Status unclear? Message Les immediately: "[Client name] — what status?"

Key Client Protocol

When Les sends "Key Client — [name]" on Slack:

  1. Log into Airtable
  2. Find the client record
  3. Pause the auto email sequence immediately
  4. Set status to Key Client
  5. Reply to Les on Slack: "Done — [name] paused and status updated."
Complete within 30 minutes. One missed pause = Key Client gets spammed. EA owns this end to end.
Escalation Rules
  • EA cannot set Closed without Les's instruction
  • EA cannot set Key Client without Les's instruction
  • EA can set all other statuses independently
  • Client escalating or frustrated → flag to Les immediately. Do not route independently
  • Any ambiguity → message Les: "[Client name] — what status?"
TOB Process — Post-Meeting to Recruiter Handover

Phase 1 — Sitting in Client Meetings: Watch how Les operates — his tone, pace, language, and how he handles objections. Never speak unless Les directs it to you. Log context into the calendar event after.

Phase 2 — After TOBs Are Sent: The assigned recruiter is the primary POC. Les steps back. EA monitors inbox for client replies.

Phase 3 — Routing Client Emails After TOBs:

Option A — Forward internally (client just sharing info): Forward to recruiter with: "FYI — client replied to Les with [summary]. You're the POC from here. No reply needed to Les."

Option B — Introduce recruiter to client (client asking a question): Reply on Les's behalf CC-ing the recruiter using Template 4.

EA Playbook 08

GTD System & Weekly Review

The operating framework that ensures nothing falls through the cracks. Capture → Clarify → Organise → Reflect → Engage.

Core Rule
Slack = conversation. Trello = execution. If it is not in Trello, it is not being done.
The 5 GTD Steps
1. Capture
Every actionable item from Slack, email, meetings, or voice notes must become a Trello card in the Inbox list. Slack is temporary. Trello is permanent.
2. Clarify
Is it actionable? No → delete or store as reference. Yes → define the outcome and the next physical action step. E.g. Outcome = "Client interview scheduled" / Next Action = "Email client 3 time options"
3. Organise
Move cards into: Inbox (captured, unprocessed) · Doing (active — keep small) · Waiting (blocked — must have follow-up date) · Parked (not now) · Done (completed)
4. Reflect
Friday Weekly Review — 45–60 min. If Friday is missed → Thursday is the fallback. Non-negotiable means it moves, it does not disappear.
5. Engage
Work from the Doing list. Keep it small. Move blocked work to Waiting immediately. Close loops daily. Avoid context switching.
Friday Weekly Review — 4 Phases

Phase 1 — Clean the Board (15 min): Process every Trello list. Inbox: clarify all. Doing: move blocked items to Waiting. Waiting: ensure every card has a follow-up date. Done: archive everything.

Phase 2 — The 6 Review Questions (15 min):

  • Q1: What is overdue or at risk right now?
  • Q2: What is blocked and needs a decision from Les?
  • Q3: What can be delegated, deleted, or parked?
  • Q4: What repeated more than twice this week? → Make it a process.
  • Q5: What should become a template, SOP, or automation?
  • Q6: What friction did I notice this week?

Phase 3 — Anticipation Loop (15 min): Scan the next 2–4 weeks. Spot problems before they happen. Add Trello cards for anything that needs action before it lands.

Phase 4 — Weekly Summary to Les (10 min): Send before end of day Friday. Use the template in the Templates tab.
